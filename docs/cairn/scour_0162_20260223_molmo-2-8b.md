## **Strands**

### **1. Modular Architecture**
- **Chasqui dispatches scouts and scourers** with minimal logic and passes most of the work to well-defined, reusable components (`scout.py`, `scourer.py`, `gleaner.py`, `analyst.py`, etc.).
- **Separation of concerns** is explicit: the coordinator manages the flow; the scorer and coverage modules analyze scout reports; the gleaner extracts claims; the analyst builds insights; the scourer targets specific files, directories, or tensors.

**Implication:** This structure supports easy extensions (e.g., adding a new analysis module) and enables experimentation with different scout/scourer models.

**What would break if this changed?**
- If the pipeline logic were merged into a monolithic file, it would be harder to debug, scale, or swap components (e.g., the model selection strategy).

### **2. Cost-Aware Dispatch**
- **Model selection is weighted by cost** (inverse of total cost per million tokens).
- Free models are favored, but a small nominal cost is assigned to them to avoid division by zero.
- The coordinator seeds the RNG for reproducibility and can filter out models that do not meet minimum context length requirements.

**Implication:** This ensures that scouts are dispatched in a way that balances cost and effectiveness, reducing unnecessary expensive dispatches.

**Open question:** How does the system handle cases where a cheap model consistently produces low-quality claims? Would manual override or reweighting be feasible?

### **3. Tensor-Based Reporting**
- All scouts and scourers output their findings in a standardized tensor format, enabling downstream analysis (gleaner, analyst, scorer, coverage).
- The tensor includes details like model, cost, run number, provenance, and content, plus “Strands,” “Declared Losses,” and “Open Questions” for structured review.

**Implication:** This design allows for automated aggregation, pattern detection, and quality filtering, as seen in the `gleaner.py` and `analyst.py` modules.

**Open question:** Are tensors designed to be human-readable and editable, or are they primarily intended for automated processing and downstream analysis? Is there a way to surface “editable” tensors directly to human reviewers?

### **4. Provenance and Accountability**
- Each scout/scourer report includes a provenance header with model ID, cost, usage, timestamp, and dispatch context.
- The Lamport bakery ensures **unique run numbers** across processes, even in concurrent environments.

**Implication:** This provides a clear audit trail for each report, making it possible to track which model reviewed a file, when, and under what circumstances.

**Open question:** Are there cases where provenance is incomplete (e.g., due to abrupt process termination), and how does the system handle incomplete or inconsistent provenance?

### **5. Coverage and Exploration**
- A **coverage tracker** scans the cairn for references to files and assigns a last-reviewed timestamp.
- Files never reviewed receive maximum weighting for dispatch, encouraging focused exploration of new or under-reviewed parts of the codebase.

**Implication:** This addresses imbalance in scout attention (common in projects with popular files), helping to surface neglected parts of the codebase.

**Open question:** What happens to files that are reviewed, but never again referenced in scout reports? Does the system consider them "covered" indefinitely, or is there a mechanism for re-exploration?

### **6. Quality Control & Verification**
- **Gleaner** extracts claims and scores them on confidence and semantic similarity.
- **Analyst** clusters claims, detects topological consensus (multiple models agree), and flags open questions or possible discrepancies for further investigation.

**Implication:** This forms a feedback loop that helps identify both high-quality insights and areas of confusion or disagreement, driving iterative improvement.

**Open question:** Are “verified claims” (topological consensus) used to update the cairn or trigger further exploration? What is the workflow for resolving discrepancies detected by the analyst?

### **7. External Exploration**
- The scourer module supports not just introspection of the project, but also scouting of external codebases, tensors, and other resources.
- This enables cross-project learning and comparison.

**Implication:** The system is designed to be **agnostic to scope**: the same dispatch logic can be used to explore internal or external targets.
