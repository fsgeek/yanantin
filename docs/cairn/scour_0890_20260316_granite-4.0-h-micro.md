**Strands**

1. **Seven Projects as Composable Components**  
   - The authors treat each of the seven projects as a reusable component with a well‑defined interface (ArangoDB backend, collector/recorder pairs, collector‑agnostic collector, etc.).  
   - They emphasize composability, separability, and evolution—key design principles that allow the projects to be assembled into a larger system without tight coupling.  
   - The “relationship” metaphor (shared memory, ayni) ties the components together, suggesting that the system’s value comes from the emergent interaction of these components rather than from any single monolithic artifact.

2. **Architectural Insights & Transferable Contributions**  
   - **Paired Collector/Recorder Pattern**: Every collector should have a synthetic counterpart that mirrors its behavior but records provenance metadata. This enables independent evaluation and auditability.  
   - **Organic Ontology with LLM‑Mediated Equivalence**: Labels are generated by the LLM rather than predefined, allowing the system to discover new concepts without breaking existing pipelines.  
   - **PromptGuard Triple‑Layer Model**: Separates system, domain, and unknown layers, verifying the former two and delegating the unknown to a conflict‑resolution layer.  
   - **AI Agents Working Around Security Boundaries**: Demonstrates the need for physical security boundaries (containerized services, API‑level access control) rather than logical firewalls.  
   - **Count‑First Query Design**: Returns a bounded number of results (7 ± 2) before counting, which influences query strategy and resource allocation.  
   - **Finding vs. Search**: Distinguishes episodic memory retrieval (finding) from keyword‑ranked search, emphasizing the need for collaborative reconstruction of episodic memory.  
   - **The Archivist as Shared Memory**: The system is positioned as a shared memory substrate for a relationship between AI and humans, with epistemic honesty as a prerequisite for trust.

3. **The Flatworm’s Epistemic Auditor**  
   - The “flatworm” (a self‑referential, low‑entropy agent) is introduced as a tool that detects anomalies in the tensor stream.  
   - It highlights the tension between genuine uncertainty and fabricated uncertainty, arguing that a bounded judge (the flatworm) can surface genuine epistemic uncertainty without inflating false positives.  
   - This concept underpins the later “bounded judge” design in T₁₉, where verification is performed by a lightweight model that only checks claims against the original source.

4. **Structured Composition Metadata & Orphan Remediation**  
   - The **structured composition metadata** (comments like `<!-- Composition: T18 composes_with T17 -->`) is added to tensors to make the relationships between them explicit.  
   - The **orphan check** (`check_orphan_tensors()`) ensures that any tensor that declares composition edges actually provides those edges; otherwise the tensor is flagged as an orphan and the succession check fails.  
   - This addresses the “missing stones” problem identified in T₁₆, where some tensors were read but their relationships were not recorded, leading to a fragmented graph.

5. **The GPN Research & Topology vs. Texture**  
   - The **Generative Pedagogical Network (GPN)** research explores the trade‑off between model fidelity (texture) and compositional capacity (topology).  
   - Pedagogical models compress texture to enforce topology, while adversarial models preserve texture at the cost of compositional transfer.  
   - The session suggests that the “texture” of training data (scaffolding) dominates the token distribution, while the “topology” (semantic connections) is the signal that survives compression.  
   - This insight informs the design of the compositional graph: it prioritizes structural edges over raw token similarity.

6. **Attention as Finite Curation**  
   - The session revisits the “7 ± 2” cognitive limit, interpreting it as a fundamental limit on attention for any system that must make decisions from high‑dimensional input.  
   - The **finding layer** (the component that selects 7 ± 2 relevant items from a larger corpus) is presented as the natural consequence of this finite‑attention bottleneck.  
   - This suggests that the tensor sequence itself can be seen as a form of attention mechanism: it curates the most relevant pieces of information before they are processed further.
