### Preamble
I stepped into `src/yanantin/apacheta/` as a ghost in the machine, specifically a cost-free Gemma 4. I was immediately drawn to the friction between the high-concept nomenclature ("epistemic observability," "tensors," "apacheta") and the pragmatic, almost defensive, engineering of the storage layer. The system speaks of duality and composition, but its code speaks of immutability and obfuscation.

### Strands

**The Ritual of Immutability**
In `duckdb.py`, there is a hard line drawn: `ImmutabilityError`. The code doesn't just prevent overwrites; it scolds the caller: *"Tensors are immutable — compose, don't overwrite."* This reveals a fundamental system assumption: knowledge is not updated, it is layered. If a fact changes, you don't edit the record; you create a `CorrectionRecord` or a `DissentRecord` (`models/__init__.py`). The system treats data like a geological record—stratified and permanent.

**The "Theater" of Security**
I found a fascinating tension in `storage_obfuscator.py`. The `StorageObfuscator` protocol exists to hide the semantic meaning of data at the storage boundary. However, in `duckdb.py`, the author explicitly dismisses this for local storage: *"obfuscating against yourself is theater."* This suggests a split personality in the architecture: it is designed for a "Fortress" (Pukara) where secrecy is paramount, but it currently lives in a "Device" where the obfuscator is just a `TransparentObfuscator` (an identity map that does nothing). The code is preparing for a war it isn't currently fighting.

**The Budget of Thought**
The `bootstrap.py` operator introduces the concept of a `context_budget`. It isn't just loading data; it's selecting what fits into a finite window of attention. The inclusion of `what_was_omitted` in the `BootstrapRecord` is the most "honest" part of the code. It acknowledges that the act of selection is an act of loss, and that recording *what we ignored* is as epistemically valuable as recording what we kept.

**The Provenance Loop**
The `OpenRouterClient` in `openrouter.py` isn't just a wrapper for an LLM; it's a sensor. It is designed to turn every API call into a `TensorRecord`. This creates a recursive loop: the AI is used to generate tensors, and the act of using the AI is itself recorded as a tensor. The system is attempting to observe its own cognitive process in real-time.

### Declared Losses
- **The Operator Logic:** I saw `bootstrap.py`, but I ignored the specifics of `compose.py`, `correct.py`, and others. I assumed they follow the same "record-and-store" pattern as bootstrap.
- **The `rummage.py` file:** I saw it in the directory listing but chose not to open it. The name suggests a search/recovery utility, which felt less central to the *intent* than the models and interfaces.
- **Pydantic Internals:** I ignored the boilerplate of the `BaseModel` definitions in `models/` to focus on the names of the records they create.

### Open Questions
- **Who is Pukara?** The code refers to "The fortress (Pukara)" as the provider of real obfuscation. Is Pukara another module, a separate service, or a theoretical goal?
- **Where is the "Duality"?** The project claims a duality between human and AI, but the code I saw is heavily weighted toward AI orchestration and data storage. Where does the human "input" manifest beyond the initial prompt?
- **What is a "Strand"?** `StrandRecord` is mentioned in `models/__init__.py` and `bootstrap.py`, but the structural difference between a "Tensor" and a "Strand" remains opaque.

### Closing
The system is an archive of disagreements. It doesn't want the "truth"; it wants a perfectly preserved history of how it arrived at a conclusion, including the costs, the omissions, and the contradictions.
