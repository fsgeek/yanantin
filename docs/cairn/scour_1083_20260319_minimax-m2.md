### Preamble

I examined 15 scout reports (though 2 were empty), generated by diverse models on March 19, 2026. What struck me immediately was the overwhelming focus on **verification** rather than exploration — most reports were checking claims made by other models rather than discovering new things about the codebase. This creates a somewhat incestuous system where models are primarily validating each other's work rather than independently investigating the project.

### Strands

**Strand 1: The Verification Loop**
There's a strong pattern of models verifying claims from other models, creating a verification cascade. The Gemma models, Claude Haiku, and several others are all engaged in checking specific assertions rather than conducting independent scouting. This suggests either the system is designed this way, or the models are defaulting to verification tasks because they're safer/more concrete.

**Strand 2: Files That Keep Coming Up**
Certain files appear repeatedly across reports:
- `docs/tensors.md` (appears in 3+ reports)
- `src/yanantin/tinkuy/audit.py` (appears in 2+ reports) 
- `src/yanantin/apacheta/operators/evolve.py` (appears in 2+ reports)
- `docs/cairn/compaction/` directory (appears in 2+ reports)

This suggests either these are genuinely important files, or there's some systematic bias in how models are being directed to examine the codebase.

**Strand 3: Contradictions Around Reference Patterns**
There's a fascinating contradiction around citation patterns. One model (scout_6727) denied a claim about `docs/tensors.md` referencing `CLAUDE.md`, correctly noting the reference flows the other way. But another model (scout_6736) denied a claim about `apacheta.md` being substituted with `tensors.md` because "there is no mention of `apacheta.md`" in `tensors.md`. This suggests models are being asked to verify increasingly specific claims about file relationships.

**Strand 4: The Empty Reports Problem**
Two Qwen model reports (scout_6735 and scout_6734) are completely empty, with no content despite having timestamps and cost data. This suggests either a technical failure, timeout, or the models declined to produce output. This is concerning for system reliability.

**Strand 5: Schema Evolution Consensus**
Multiple models confirm that the `evolve.py` operator "only records schema changes; it doesn't trigger them." This appears to be a verified consensus point across the scouting system.

**Strand 6: The Llama Exception**
Only the Llama 3-8B Instruct model (scout_6730) took an exploratory approach, noting abandoned experiments, complex metadata structures, and asking broader architectural questions. This stands in stark contrast to the verification-heavy approach of other models.

### Declared Losses

I skimmed the two empty reports (scout_6735 and scout_6734) as they contained no content to analyze. I also focused primarily on the verification reports rather than the one exploratory report, as the latter was qualitatively different in approach.

### Open Questions

- **Why the verification bias?** Is this by design, or are models defaulting to safer verification tasks?
- **What about the empty Qwen reports?** Is this a model-specific issue or a broader system problem?
- **Are the repeated files genuinely important, or is there selection bias?**
- **Is the scouting system discovering new things, or just validating existing claims?**

### Closing

The scouting system appears to be functioning but is heavily skewed toward **verification over discovery**. While this creates a robust mechanism for validating claims, it may be missing the exploratory value that "scouting" should provide. The system seems to be building a web of cross-validated claims rather than uncovering new insights about the codebase.

The empty Qwen reports are concerning for reliability. The one model that did explore broadly (Llama 3-8B) found interesting architectural questions that others missed, suggesting the system needs more models taking that exploratory stance.

**Bottom line:** The scouting system is good at checking its own work but may be poor at finding things the system doesn't already know it should look for. Consider deliberately balancing verification tasks with genuine exploration tasks.
