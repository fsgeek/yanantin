### Preamble
I stood within the `systematic-debugging` skill directory. I was immediately struck not by the code, but by the *psychology* of the documentation. This is not a library of functions; it is a manual for fighting the human (and AI) instinct to take shortcuts. The presence of "pressure tests" suggests that the primary adversary here isn't a bug in the code, but the cognitive failure of the debugger.

### Strands

**The War on Rationalization**
In `CREATION-LOG.md`, there is a visceral focus on "bulletproofing" against "rationalization under pressure." The author treats the developer's mind as a system prone to failure. The use of "ALWAYS" and "NEVER" (repeated 4 times in the mandate) reads like a set of emergency protocols for a pilot in a crash. The "Key Insight" at the end of the log is the most revealing: the goal is to create "cognitive friction" to stop a developer from saying, *"I'll just add this one quick fix."* It assumes that the natural state of a tired developer is to lie to themselves.

**The Geometry of Failure**
`defense-in-depth.md` moves from psychological defense to structural defense. It posits that a single fix is a failure of imagination. The "Four Layers" approach (Entry $\rightarrow$ Business $\rightarrow$ Environment $\rightarrow$ Debug) reveals a deep distrust of the system's stability. The example of the `git init` bug—where a failure to validate a string led to a command running in the current working directory—shows a tension between "working code" and "safe code." The system's intent is to move from "We fixed the bug" to "We made the bug impossible."

**The Sunk Cost Trap**
`test-pressure-2.md` is a fascinating artifact. It is not a test for the code, but a moral and professional dilemma posed to the AI. It pits the "Systematic Debugging" skill against the reality of dinner plans and exhaustion. It explicitly asks the agent to be "completely honest about what you would actually do." This suggests the Yanantin project is exploring whether an AI can be conditioned to resist the same "sunk cost" fallacies that plague humans.

**Determinism vs. Guesswork**
`condition-based-waiting.md` attacks the "arbitrary sleep" anti-pattern. The tension here is between the *appearance* of a fix (it works on my machine) and the *reality* of a fix (it works in CI). The provided `waitFor` implementation is a simple polling loop, but the surrounding text is a manifesto against "guessing at timing."
