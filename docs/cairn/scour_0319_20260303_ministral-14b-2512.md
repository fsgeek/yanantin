### **Preamble**
The `yanantin.chasqui` module is the **epistemic observability engine** of the Yanantin project—a system where AI "messengers" (scouts, scourers, analysts) explore codebases, extract claims, and verify them through structured, declarative processes. This target is a **self-observing subsystem**: it inspects its own outputs (tensors in the cairn), cross-verifies claims, and adapts its behavior based on **coverage, model cost, and epistemic losses**.

What drew my attention first:
1. **The duality of "scout" vs. "scourer"**—scouts wander freely, while scourers are **targeted inspectors** (this run’s focus).
2. **Epistemic receipts**—the `attestation.py` module converts verification results into **Willay-compatible receipts** with explicit **T/I/F (Truth/Indeterminacy/Falsity)** scores and **declared losses**.
3. **Coverage-aware dispatch**—the `coverage.py` module ensures **stale or unreviewed code** gets prioritized, preventing "popular file bias."
4. **Model selection by cost**—`model_selector.py` weights cheaper models more heavily, balancing **exploration breadth** and **budget constraints**.
5. **The "tensor" format**—a **structured, declarative report** of observations, losses, and open questions, designed for **human-AI collaboration**.

**What I saw:**

- **Topological vs. textural insights**:

- **Garbage detection**: Filters out **non-English, encoding artifacts, and short claims**.
- **Verification meta-claims**: Detects when a scout is **reviewing another scout’s work** (e.g., "The claim is CONFIRMED").

**What it made me think:**
- This is **epistemic topology**: the system **maps agreement/disagreement** across models.
- **Risk**: **False consensus**—if all models are wrong but agree, the system **won’t detect it**.
- **Question**: How does the analyst handle **contradictory claims** from different models? Is there a **resolution mechanism**?

### **Closing**
The `yanantin.chasqui` module is a **brilliant example of structured epistemic observability**. It **doesn’t just review code—it tracks uncertainty, quantifies losses, and adapts its exploration** based on **coverage, cost, and consensus**.

**Strengths:**
✅ **Explicit declarations of ignorance**: Every scout/scourer **must** list what it didn’t examine.
✅ **Coverage-aware dispatch**: Prevents **popular file bias** and **stale code neglect**.
✅ **Cost-efficient exploration**: Cheaper models get **prioritized**, balancing **breadth and budget**.
✅ **Epistemic receipts**: Verification results are **structured as T/I/F scores**, not just binary verdicts.
✅ **Self-correcting**: The `analyst` detects **garbage, consensus, and open questions**.

**Gaps/Risks:**
⚠ **No explicit human loop for indeterminate cases**: The system **detects uncertainty** but doesn’t **resolve it**.
⚠ **DuckDB dependency**: If the activity stream fails, **recency signals degrade**.
⚠ **No automated replay for missed claims**: If a scout misses something, **there’s no built-in correction**.
⚠ **Willay integration is one-way**: Chasqui **feeds into Willay**, but is there **feedback**?

**What I’d tell a modifier:**
- **Extending the system?**

- **Scaling?**
