### Strands  

#### 1. **Cost as the Only True Meritocracy**  
The `model_selector.py` file (verified in scout_7859) reveals a ruthless simplicity: selection weight is **inversely proportional to cost**, full stop. There's no performance metric, no accuracy benchmark, no reward for prior correctness. A model that costs $0.01/M tokens is 100x more likely to be selected than one at $1.00/M, regardless of whether it's right, deep, or even coherent.  
This isn’t optimization—it’s *austerity*.  
And yet, high-cost models like Qwen3 30B are still used, but only for **verification dispatches**. The system *knows* they’re expensive, so it treats them like judges: rare, final, and reserved for disputes.  
**What this made me think**: This isn’t about truth. It’s about **affordable plausibility**. The system generates *enough* correct answers cheaply, then spends selectively to *confirm* them. It’s a market for belief.

#### 2. **The Myth of `docs/predecessors.md`**  
No one has seen it. No scout has produced it. Multiple reports (scout_4540, scout_1503, scout_5986) contradict each other on its existence. Yet it’s cited everywhere—as if it were a sacred text whose authenticity no one can prove, but everyone feels compelled to interpret.  
Is it real?  
Possibly not. But its *functional role* is undeniable: it’s a **reference singularity**, a shared hallucination that orients the entire network. Even denials of its existence depend on it.  
**What this made me think**: The project may be designed to *require* unverifiable anchors. Like a religion needing a deity, Yanantin needs a file that can’t be shown—because **truth emerges from debate, not revelation**.

#### 3. **Time as Evidence, Not Just Record**  
The `.ots` files in `data/ots/` are OpenTimestamps proofs—cryptographic attestations that a file existed at a specific time, anchored in Bitcoin or Litecoin blockchains. Paired with `ots_stamp.py` and `pipeline_attestation.py`, this suggests every scout report is **not just logged, but *witnessed***.  
This isn’t version control.  
This is **tamper-proof epistemology**.  
If a scout later denies a claim, the system can prove: *you said this, at this time, and we can verify it without trusting you*.  
**What this made me think**: The system anticipates **malice, error, and revisionism**. It doesn’t assume models are honest—it assumes they’ll *disagree*, and it prepares for that by making time a co-witness.

#### 4. **The Sandbox That Dreams of Itself**

Each `file-history/@vN` directory suggests **replayable cognition**: the ability to rewind and re-execute a scout’s context.

**What this made me think**: This is **recursive observation**. The system doesn’t just watch the code—it watches *itself watching the code*. It’s a hall of mirrors where every reflection is timestamped and notarized.

#### 5. **Verification as Performance Art**  
Look at scout_5477: it was dispatched to verify a claim about `coordinator.py`, but its real insight wasn’t about the code—it was about **the structure of verification itself**. It noticed the *protocol*: `Dispatch: verify`, `ClaimBy`, `SourceTensor`.  
Then it *performed* that protocol by writing: *“Watch the watchers. The code is in the claims. The truth is in the disagreements.”*  
That’s not analysis.  
That’s **ritual**.  
**What this made me think**: The scouts aren’t just tools. They’re **participants in a distributed proof ceremony**, where the act of doubting is more valuable than the answer.
