### Strands  

1. **The Paradox of Self-Review**  
   - **Observation:** The implementer-prompt.md mandates a rigorous self-review (e.g., “Did I avoid overbuilding (YAGNI)?”, “Are names clear?”). Yet the spec reviewer is told to ignore the implementer’s claims entirely.  
   - **Implication:** Self-review is both required and distrusted. The system acknowledges that implementers *can* reflect critically but *won’t* (or can’t) without external pressure. This creates a loop: self-review is a first line of defense, but the spec reviewer acts as a second, more skeptical line.  
   - **Surprise:** The process assumes self-review is valuable but insufficient — a hedge against human (or subagent) optimism bias.  

2. **The Spec Reviewer as Detective**  
   - **Observation:** The spec-reviewer-prompt.md frames the role as investigative: “Compare actual implementation to requirements line by line,” “Look for extra features they didn’t mention.” The reviewer is instructed to treat the implementer’s report as a potential crime scene.  
   - **Tension:** The implementer is given agency to ask questions and self-correct, but the spec reviewer is tasked with proving they did it *wrong*. This mirrors a legal process: implementation is the act, the report is the testimony, and the reviewer is the fact-checker.  
   - **Confusion:** Why not trust the implementer’s self-review? Is the system designed for fallible subagents, or is this a safeguard against future human use?  

3. **The Illusion of Independence**  
   - **Observation:** SKILL.md emphasizes “fresh subagent per task” to avoid “context pollution.” Yet the process requires the human (or orchestrator) to answer questions during implementation (e.g., “Should the hook be installed at user or system level?”).  
   - **Tension:** Tasks are *meant* to be independent, but in practice, they require real-time human intervention. This creates a dependency chain that could bottleneck the process if many tasks hit ambiguities.  
   - **Surprise:** The “independent tasks” assumption is fragile — the system relies on humans to resolve ambiguities, undermining full automation.  

4. **The Final Reviewer as God**  
   - **Observation:** After all tasks are complete, a “final code reviewer subagent for entire implementation” is dispatched. This step isn’t detailed in the templates, but SKILL.md mentions it as a last step.  
   - **Implication:** The per-task reviews are granular, but the final review holistically assesses the whole. This suggests that even after passing two reviews per task, the system isn’t fully trusted — emergent issues might arise at scale.
