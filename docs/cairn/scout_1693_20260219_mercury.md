**Preamble**  
I arrived at the Yanantin codebase through the `docs/cairn` directory, a dense forest of scout reports that each start with a header describing the run, model, cost, and usage. The first thing that caught my eye was the **consistent use of “tensor” as a data unit** – from `src/yanantin/apacheta/config.py` (the `ConfigTensor` class) to the numerous `TensorRecord` objects referenced in the scout reports. The reports also repeatedly mention *provenance*, *epistemic clarity*, and *composition operators*, suggesting a design that treats knowledge as a **relational, immutable field** rather than a mutable document.

The `src/yanantin/chasqui` package is the glue that ties together scouts, analysts, and the underlying storage backends. The `scout.py` file, in particular, provides prompt‑building functions but does not show any actual model calls, hinting that the model invocation is handled elsewhere (perhaps in a separate orchestrator or an external service). This separation of concerns is a recurring theme.

**Declared Losses**

1. **ArangoDB Backend** – I did not open `src/yanantin/apacheta/backends/arango.py`. While the directory is claimed to contain multiple backends, I have no evidence of its implementation or usage.
2. **Full Tensor Lifecycle** – I did not trace a tensor from ingestion (`ingest` module) through storage to retrieval. The `rummage` and `materialize` modules were out of scope.
3. **.ots File Structure** – I could not inspect the binary `.ots` files; their format and validation logic remain unknown.
4. **Cost‑Weighted Sampling** – The mechanism for selecting scouts based on cost is mentioned in the README but not explored in the code I examined.
5. **Provenance Enforcement** – While the `ProvenanceEnvelope` class exists, I did not verify how it is enforced or validated at runtime.

**Open Questions**

- How are the `.ots` files generated, validated, and consumed by the system? Are they signed snapshots of tensors?
- What is the exact workflow for invoking a model (e.g., `qwen/qwen3-235b`) from a scout? Which orchestrator or service performs the HTTP request?
- How does the `correct()` operator preserve both original and corrected claims? Does it create a new tensor or update metadata?
- What happens when a scout’s claim is never corrected? Is there a timeout or meta‑scout that checks for stale claims?
- How is the `is_byok` flag used in practice? Does it affect whether a claim is accepted into the knowledge graph?
- Are there any tests or runtime checks that ensure the `CompositionEdge` ordering metadata is respected during composition?

**Closing**

The Yanantin project is built around **immutable, relational tensors** that carry provenance, cost, and epistemic metadata. Scouts act as impartial observers, building prompts and logging claims, while auditors and analysts later verify and correct those claims. The system’s modular backends and the file‑tree ritual ensure a consistent ground truth across models. However, several pieces—particularly the `.ots` files, model invocation layer, and provenance enforcement—remain opaque from my current viewpoint. My next steps would be to locate the orchestrator that sends prompts to external models, inspect the ArangoDB backend, and explore the `.ots` format to understand how signed snapshots integrate into the tensor graph.
