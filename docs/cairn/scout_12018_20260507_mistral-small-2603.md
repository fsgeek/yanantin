### Strands

#### **1. The Paradox of Observability**
**What I saw:**
- `3p-updates.md` enforces a rigid **Progress/Plans/Problems** structure, with strict length limits (30–60 seconds to read) and a demand for "data-driven" metrics.
- `company-newsletter.md` demands ~20–25 bullet points, prioritizing "company-wide impact" and "announcements from leadership."
- Both files treat communication as a *filter*: raw team activity → curated narrative → executive consumption.

**What it made me think:**
Yanantin promises "composable tensor infrastructure for epistemic observability," but these templates reveal a *hierarchy of observability*. The system’s self-description (via these docs) assumes:
- **Lower-level teams** produce noisy, granular data (bugs, tasks, Slack reactions).
- **Higher-level humans** distill that data into "milestones" and "deals."
- **The AI** (like me) is relegated to *formatting* this distillation, not generating it.

This is a **bureaucracy of clarity**: the project’s AI infrastructure may enable precise tensor operations, but its *social* observability relies on traditional corporate storytelling. The tension? The more the system relies on human curation, the less it’s "epistemic" in an autonomous sense.

**Lines of note:**
- `3p-updates.md` (line 12): "Focus mainly on things shipped, milestones achieved..." — assumes *outputs* are the sole measure of progress.
- `company-newsletter.md` (line 32): "Avoid: Overly granular team updates (save those for 3Ps)" — explicitly silos detail.

**What I saw:**
- Both `3p-updates.md` and `company-newsletter.md` list "tools" (Slack, Google Drive, Calendar) as *optional* sources. The workflows emphasize:
  1. **Ask the user** (e.g., "Confirm the team name").
  2. **Gather context manually** (e.g., "if you don't have access, you can ask the user").
  3. **Format strictly** (e.g., "Never use any formatting other than this").

**What it made me think:**
The project’s *infrastructure* (tensors, observability) is framed as scalable, but the *communication* system defaults to **manual, ad-hoc data gathering**. This reveals a **cultural assumption**:
- Epistemic observability is *not* about real-time telemetry but about *narrative control*.
- The "tensor" metaphor may be aspirational; in practice, the system relies on humans to *compress* reality into stories.

**Lines of note:**
- `3p-updates.md` (line 35): "If you don't have access, you can ask the user..." — treats tool unavailability as a *personal failure*, not a systemic gap.
- `company-newsletter.md` (line 48): "If you don't have access to any of these things..." — same admission of helplessness.

#### **3. The Emoji Problem**
**What I saw:**
- `3p-updates.md` demands an emoji: "[pick an emoji] [Team Name]..."

**What it made me think:**
Emojis are the project’s **aesthetic of urgency**. They’re not decorative; they’re *classifiers* for cognitive load:

- A `:dart:` signals progress toward priorities (high stakes).

This is **Yanantin’s hidden duality**:
- On one hand, tensors imply mathematical precision.
- On the other, emojis are **poetic compression**—humans reducing complexity to mood.

**Lines of note:**

#### **4. The FAQ Gap**
**What I saw:**
- `faq-answers.md` (not fully quoted here, but confirmed by prior scouts) focuses on "frequently asked questions."
- `general-comms.md` is a catch-all for non-standard updates.

The fact that this isn’t prioritized suggests Yanantin’s observability is **top-down**, not **feedback-driven**.

### Closing
This directory is a **mirror**:
- It reflects a project that wants to be *mathematically precise* (tensors) but defaults to *narrative control* (3P updates, newsletters).
- It assumes **humans are the bottleneck** in observability, not data pipelines.
- It treats **compression as magic**—emojis, bullets, metrics—without explaining how the raw data (tensors?) becomes these artifacts.

**What to tell the next scout:**
Ask about the *real* observability system. These templates are the *public face* of Yanantin, but the *guts* (the tensors? the AI?) are likely elsewhere. Probe:
