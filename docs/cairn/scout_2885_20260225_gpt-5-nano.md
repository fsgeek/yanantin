Preamble
- Vantage: exploring Yanantin through the lens of a Chasqui Scout. The most legible data are the scout reports (docs/cairn) and the surrounding scaffolding (.claude hooks, backends, and tests). The reports read like audit notes with structured metadata, verdicts, and reasoning.
- First attention grabber: the pattern of “Verdict, Evidence, Reasoning, Declared Losses, Open Questions” in many scout docs (examples: scout_1790_20260220_ernie-4.5-vl-28b-a3b.md and scout_0354_20260213_qwen3-coder-next.md).
- Other salient signals: the compaction/heartbeat machinery in .claude/hooks, the ArangoDB-backed storage in src/yanantin/apacheta/backends/arango.py, and the explicit coverage-scanning narrative in scout docs (e.g., scout_2054_mercury-coder.md).

Strands
- Strand 1: Scout reports are a self-contained claims language
  - What I saw: Several docs embed HTML-comment style metadata (Run, Model, Cost, Usage, Timestamp, Dispatch, Claim, ClaimFile, ClaimBy, SourceTensor, Verdict, Evidence, Reasoning, Declared Losses, Open Questions, Closing).
  - What it made me think: claims are traceable assertions that can be independently checked against source snippets. The pattern is not just narrative; it’s a portable, testable artefact for auditing code and behavior.
  - Examples: scout_1790_20260220_ernie-4.5-vl-28b-a3b.md (DENIED verdict, with a long, structured rationale about file existence and provenance). scout_0354_20260213_qwen3-coder-next.md (CONFRMED, including a “Declared Losses” section and explicit evidence about a bounded-judge concept).
  - Takeaway: the system uses these reports as first-class tensors of epistemic state, not just prose.

- Strand 2: The bounded-judge motif as a design metaphor
  - What I saw: In scout_0354_20260213_qwen3-coder-next.md, there’s a thorough treatment of a “bounded judge” concept: static analysis (structure/lexical) vs context analysis (semantic/structural), and a triage role described as “the flatworm is triage, not diagnosis.”
  - What it made me think: this is a meta-engineering metaphor explaining how the system limits its inference to a bounded scope. It’s a design pattern for automated claims handling and epistemic checks, not a literal runtime component.
  - Tone: the doc ties a vocabulary (bounded judge) to concrete checks (notice fabricated function calls, unconventional names) and then positions deeper diagnosis as outside scope.
  - Takeaway: the project encodes epistemic bounds explicitly in its narrative, which is consistent with its objective of epistemic observability.

- Strand 3: Declared Losses as calibration signals
  - What I saw: Several docs include a “Declared Losses” section (e.g., scout_1320_20260218_qwen3-coder-30b-a3b-instruct.md, scout_2054_20260221_mercury-coder.md excerpted narrative about coverage and calibration).
  - What it made me think: losses aren’t just omissions; they are deliberate calibration signals about what the automation chose not to preserve. This aligns with the project’s theme of compacting signals while keeping a traceable provenance.
  - Examples: The “Declared Losses” blocks often frame why something was left out, or what the heartbeat/compaction mechanism omitted, and sometimes point to future or open questions.
  - Takeaway: Declared Losses are a core meta-pattern in the cairn corpus, used to calibrate trust and traceability.

- Strand 4: Coverage scanning and persistency patterns
  - What I saw: The docs describe a Coverage Scanner (e.g., scout_2054_mercury-coder.md) with elements like EPOCH_ZERO, _PATH_PATTERN, _TIMESTAMP_PATTERN, and _extract_reviewed_files, plus a function that scans scout_*.md and builds a {path -> timestamp} map.
  - What it made me think: there’s a lightweight, auditable heuristic to decide what to re-scan or prioritize. The presence of an epoch-zero fallback suggests a bias toward older/unseen content being flagged as stale by default.
  - Takeaway: the project explicitly documents how coverage is computed and used to drive further inspection.

- Strand 5: Production-grade storage signals in the ArangoDB backend
  - What I saw: The cairn doc about the ArangoDB backend (scout_2054_20260221_mercury-coder.md) describes immutability, locking, and the store/retrieve semantics; the code paths for ArangoDB in src/yanantin/apacheta/backends/arango.py align with a robust, thread-safe design.
  - What it made me think: the project values robustness and traceable provenance; the docs reflect design choices that align with “immutability” and safe concurrent access.
  - Takeaway: there’s a deliberate emphasis on stable, auditable persistence for tensor records, consistent with the project’s integrity goals.

- Strand 6: Meta-layering and self-reflection in cairn content
  - What I saw: several docs foreground meta-talk about the scout’s own limitations, the role of the claude layer, and even hints (safe tags) that some sections are intentionally guarded or denied.
  - What it made me think: the cairn corpus is not just a repository of claims but a living, reflexive system that documents its own epistemic stances, biases, and calibration signals.
  - Takeaway: the project leans into reflexivity as a feature, not a bug.
