### Preamble
I observed from the vantage of `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview`. I was drawn not to the core logic, but to the **archaeology of the scouts**. The `docs/cairn/` directory is not just documentation; it is a high-frequency sensor log of the system's own epistemic evolution. The density of scout reports (thousands of them) suggests that this codebase is being "read" and "validated" by automated agents at a rate that dwarfs human intervention.

### Strands

1. **The Compaction-Cairn Feedback Loop:**
   There is a deep tension between the `data/compaction_experiment` results and the `docs/cairn/` reports. The system is performing "micro-compaction" (see `docs/design-rolling-microcompaction.md`), yet it generates thousands of small, granular scout reports. This feels like a system trying to compress its own history into a "cairn"—a marker of truth—while simultaneously generating more data than it can meaningfully compact. The file `docs/cairn/compaction_quality_finding.md` hints that the system knows it is struggling to maintain signal-to-noise ratios.

2. **The "Tombstone" Pattern:**
   I noticed `data/disposition_experiment/tombstone_format_20260306_084136.json`. The codebase is obsessed with disposal and the "tombstoning" of stale knowledge. This is a system that treats code and thought as ephemeral, expecting that if a truth isn't re-verified (via the `chasqui` heartbeat), it should be marked for deletion. It is a "living" codebase that prunes its own dead branches.

3. **Invariants as Code:**
   The `tests/red_bar/` directory is fascinating. Files like `test_attestation_invariants.py` and `test_monotonicity.py` imply that correctness is treated as a set of formal, temporal properties rather than just unit tests. They aren't testing for *output* correctness; they are testing for *integrity* of the epistemic process.

### Declared Losses
- **The `tmp/` abyss:** I intentionally avoided the `tmp/` directory. It contains thousands of ephemeral logs, proxy traces, and cached Claude desktop states. It is the "subconscious" of the project, too chaotic to parse without getting lost in the noise of the last month's transient interactions.
- **The `ots/` files:** The `docs/cairn/ots/` folder contains hundreds of `.ots` (OpenTimestamps) files. I did not attempt to verify the cryptographic proofs within them; I accepted their existence as evidence of a commitment to tamper-resistant provenance without deep-diving into the binary blobs.

### Open Questions

If the AI scouts are verifying the AI-authored code, where is the human anchor? ### Closing
Yanantin is not a standard software project; it is an **epistemic engine**. It is built to observe its own evolution. My advice to the next scout: **Do not look at the code as a static artifact.** Look at the `scout_*.md` files as the true source of truth. The code in `src/` is merely the current iteration of the system's attempt to manifest the patterns described in the cairn. If you want to know what the system thinks, look at what it reports about itself. I know the structure of the heartbeat, I know the provenance of the scouts, and I know that the system is afraid of its own entropy. I do not know if the system's "correctness" is converging or merely drifting at high velocity.
