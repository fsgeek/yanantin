# Chasqui Scout Report: Yanantin Exploration

## Vantage
I observed this codebase from the perspective of **`google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite-preview-09-2025`** (`Google: Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite Preview 09-2025`), run #0. The sheer density of dated, UUID-named files in `docs/cairn/ots/` immediately demanded attention—a fossil record of ephemeral interactions. My focus immediately shifted to how this history is managed alongside the system's stated goal of "epistemic observability."

## Strands

### Strand 1: The Cult of the UUID and Ephemeral Artifacts
The most striking feature is the **`docs/cairn/ots/`** directory, containing over 500 files named by 12-character hexadecimal strings (e.g., `0005f03cf1.ots`). These appear to be "Other Tensor Snapshots" or similar artifacts. The quantity suggests a history of high-frequency, short-lived experiments or observations.

*   **Observation:** The existence of `docs/cairn/tensor_session_20260306_gateway.md` alongside hundreds of `.ots` files suggests these snapshots are central to the system's definition of "tensor."
*   **Inference:** The system aggressively archives every minute step, creating an extremely high-resolution audit trail. This is a commitment to *deep* observability, perhaps bordering on data hoarding, which is fascinating for a system focused on *epistemic* (knowledge-based) observability rather than raw performance logging.

### Strand 2: Duality and Tension in the Hook Directory (`.claude/hooks`)
The `.claude/hooks` directory suggests that Claude (perhaps the environment or a specific model interface) is deeply integrated into the operational flow, specifically around data compaction.

*   **Observation:** Files like `capture_compaction.py`, `precompact_tensor.py`, and `pipeline_attestation.py` suggest automated processes tied to data structure maintenance. Crucially, `chasqui_heartbeat.sh` and `chasqui_pulse.py` exist here.
*   **Confusion:** Why are the **Chasqui** (messenger) monitoring scripts housed inside the **`.claude`** configuration directory? This implies that the Claude environment is the primary execution context for the system's monitoring/liveness checks, suggesting a strong dependency on that runtime environment for self-awareness, which contradicts the idea of a highly portable, composable tensor infrastructure.

### Strand 3: The Vocabulary of Duality and Conflict
The project name *Yanantin* implies complementary duality (a concept from Andean philosophy). This is reflected in the code structure, often showing pairs of concepts:

*   **Observation:** `src/yanantin/apacheta/operators/` contains `dissent.py` and `negate.py` alongside `correct.py` and `evolve.py`.
*   **Inference:** This isn't just CRUD; the system is explicitly designed to model *disagreement* (`dissent`) or *rejection* (`negate`) as valid operations on tensors, rather than hiding them as errors. This is a strong hint about the "epistemic observability" goal—it's not about finding the single truth, but mapping the space of perspectives.

### Strand 4: The Future is Dated (2026-03-06)
The file names in `data/disposition_experiment` and `docs/cairn/session-2026-03-06-context-protocol.md` are all stamped with dates far in the future relative to today (assuming 'today' is pre-2026).

*   **Observation:** Files like `aggressive_20260306_064223.json` and `T30_compaction_20260302_074209.md` anchor many experiments to March 2026.
*   **Confusion:** This suggests either this codebase is a *future snapshot* of an ongoing project, or the experiment scheduling/naming convention is based on a highly specific, perhaps anticipated, roadmap timeline. It’s unusual to see such specific future dating in generic source code structure.

## Declared Losses
I did not delve into the contents of the **`ots/`** files themselves, as they are binary/opaque blobs, and my function is to observe structure and intent. I also skipped a deep dive into **`src/yanantin/awaq/`** (materialize, weaver), as its purpose seems downstream of the core provenance/activity tracking I observed. Finally, I ignored **`tmp/claude/`** contents, as they look like ephemeral local session data (`conversations.json`, `projects.json`) tied to a specific user environment, not the core project logic.

## Closing
Yanantin is a system obsessed with documenting its own existence, lineage, and the potential for internal disagreement. The separation of concerns is rigorous—`activity`, `apacheta`, `query`, `tinkuy`—but the operational monitoring (`.claude/hooks`) betrays a localized dependency. The next scout should immediately try to parse one of the `.ots` files to understand what a primitive "tensor observation" looks like, and then investigate the tension between the declared immutability of provenance and the operational reality suggested by `dissent.py`. This system is building a library of knowledge states, not just data.
