### Declared Losses  

1. **Scope limitation** – The target intentionally excludes `docs/` and internal build artifacts (`__pycache__`, `.git`, etc.).  
2. **Depth limitation** – The file‑tree walker is capped at four levels deep; deeper modules (e.g., `src/yanantin/collector`) are not explored.  
3. **Temporal drift** – The system does not verify that the source files have not changed between claim extraction and verification.  
4. **Cross‑model verification** – The verifier only checks claims against the same model that produced them; cross‑model verification is not implemented.  
5. **Semantic novelty** – The analysis deliberately **omits semantic novelty** (e.g., “novel patterns”) because it requires a human judge or a more sophisticated reasoning model.  

### Open Questions  

1. **Cross‑model consistency** – How often do claims about the same file come from different models? Are there systematic disagreements?  
2. **Garbage rate** – What proportion of claims are flagged as garbage? Is there a systematic source (e.g., certain models, certain file types)?  
3. **Coverage gaps** – Which files have never been reviewed? Are they truly “never reviewed” or are they hidden in sub‑directories?  
4. **Quality distribution** – What is the distribution of `quality_score` across models? Are there outliers that deserve special attention?  
5. **Verification ledger integrity** – How is the ledger’s hash‑chaining maintained? Are there gaps in the chain?  

### Closing Impression  

The `src/yanantin/chasqui` package is a **well‑structured, cost‑aware scouting system** that produces deterministic, markdown‑formatted tensors. It balances cost efficiency with quality by weighting models inversely to their pricing, and it provides rich provenance metadata that can be leveraged for downstream verification, scoring, and topology analysis. The design deliberately limits scope and depth to keep the focus on the public codebase while still exposing enough structural information for meaningful introspection. The only gaps are the **absence of cross‑model verification** and **semantic novelty detection**, which could be addressed in future extensions. Overall, the system is ready for systematic introspection, cost‑aware dispatch, and epistemic record‑keeping.
