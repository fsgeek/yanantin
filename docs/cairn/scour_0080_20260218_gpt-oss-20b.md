**Preamble**  
I examined all 15 scout tensors (runs 1298 – 1284). The collection is a mix of deep architectural surveys, targeted verification reports, and a handful of “hallucinated” outputs. A dominant theme is the Yanantin project’s *modular, immutable, provenance‑centric* design, with a clear separation between scouting, scoring, auditing, and ingestion. What stands out is the consistency of this narrative across models, the repeated emphasis on “blueprint verification” and “schema evolution”, and the frequent but unsubstantiated denial of the existence of `docs/predecessors.md`. Few reports touch on runtime performance, concurrency, or the mechanics of the unseen `scourer.py` module.

### Open Questions (require code inspection or execution)

1. **Scourer‑Scout Interaction** – Does a `scourer.py` module exist, and if so, how is it orchestrated with `scout.py`? What is its responsibility (e.g., post‑processing, scoring)?
2. **Schema Evolution Enforcement** – `evolve.py` only logs changes; where are the runtime checks that reject or migrate tensors with outdated schemas?
3. **Compaction Strategy** – The hook `capture_compaction.py` suggests a mechanism; what compression format is used, and how is it applied to the `.ots` files?
4. **Concurrency & Immutability Guarantees** – How do backends enforce immutability under concurrent writes, especially the ArangoDB backend?
5. **Performance Benchmarks** – What is the throughput of ingesting a batch of tensors, and how does content‑addressable storage impact I/O?
6. **Security Policies** – How are least‑privilege and immutability enforced at the database level (e.g., ArangoDB ACLs)?

### Closing  
The scouting system is **finding useful structural insights**: modularity, immutability, provenance, audit, and schema evolution are well‑documented. However, it is **missing critical runtime and operational observations**. The absence of `scourer.py` verification and the lack of evidence for conflict resolution or performance metrics suggest that the current scouting pipeline focuses predominantly on static code analysis and documentation, not on end‑to‑end system behavior.  

**Recommendations for maintainers**

1. **Add a dedicated `scourer` component** (or clarify its absence) and document its orchestration with `scout`.  
2. **Expose runtime audit logs** and provide automated checks against the blueprint to surface discrepancies early.  
3. **Document and test schema evolution enforcement** so that tensors with outdated schemas are caught at ingestion.  
4. **Implement and publish performance benchmarks** for ingestion, query, and compaction across backends.  
5. **Include security and least‑privilege tests** in the core test suite to assure that immutability is enforced beyond the code layer.  

Overall, the scouting reports give a solid **static picture** of Yanantin’s architecture, but the **dynamic health** of the system remains unverified. Extending the scouting scope to cover these operational aspects will provide a more comprehensive health assessment.
