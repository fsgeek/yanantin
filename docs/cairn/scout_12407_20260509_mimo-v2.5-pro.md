### Preamble
I observed from within the `feature-dev/agents/` directory, a triad of markdown files defining specialized AI agents for software development. What drew my attention first was the identical toolset across all three agents, despite their profoundly different missions. The repetition felt less like redundancy and more like a declaration of a shared, constrained universe of action.

### Strands

**1. The Tyranny of the Identical Toolkit**
All three agents (`code-reviewer.md` line 8, `code-explorer.md` line 8, `code-architect.md` line 8) declare the exact same tools: `Glob, Grep, LS, Read, NotebookRead, WebFetch, TodoWrite, WebSearch, KillShell, BashOutput`. This is the most striking feature. It implies a core architectural assumption: that the fundamental acts of *reviewing*, *exploring*, and *architecting* are, at the tool level, indistinguishable. The differentiation is purely procedural, encoded in the prompt. The `KillShell` and `BashOutput` tools, present in a code *reviewer*, are particularly surprising—they grant destructive power to an agent whose ideal output is a static report. This suggests the system prioritizes uniform capability over role-specific safety constraints.

**2. Confidence as a Filter, Not a Metric**
The `code-reviewer.md` (lines 22-32) doesn't just use confidence scoring; it uses it as a brutal filter. The threshold is ≥80. The scale is defined with such granularity (0, 25, 50, 75, 100) that the 75 ("Highly confident... Important and will directly impact functionality") is explicitly discarded. This is a system that would rather miss a real issue than report a false positive. The philosophy is one of extreme precision, possibly at the cost of recall. It values the developer's attention as a scarce resource to be guarded, not filled.

**3. The Architect's Decisive Isolation**
The `code-architect.md` (line 17) commands: "Make decisive choices - pick one approach and commit." This is a direct instruction to avoid the exploratory, comparative analysis that the `code-explorer` is built for. The architect is a *closer*. It is designed to produce a singular, actionable blueprint. This creates a fascinating tension: the explorer generates a map of possibilities, while the architect is forced to choose a single path through it. Their collaboration would be a dialectic—thesis (exploration), antithesis (analysis), synthesis (architecture).

**4. The Ghost of `CLAUDE.md`**
Every agent references a `CLAUDE.md` file as the source of project guidelines (`code-reviewer.md` line 16, `code-architect.md` line 13). This file is the agents' constitution, the ultimate authority. Yet it is not in this directory. It is a phantom reference, an external dependency that defines all local behavior. The agents are interpreters of a law they do not contain. This makes their operation context-dependent in a way that is not self-evident from their own definitions.

### Declared Losses
I did not examine the `KillShell` and `BashOutput` tools themselves. Their names are evocative and terrifying, but their implementation is outside my vantage. I also chose not to speculate on the model `sonnet`—its capabilities are assumed, not analyzed. The color assignments (red, yellow, green) are noted but not interpreted; they may be for UI rendering or symbolic meaning, but I have no data.

### Open Questions
1.  **Orchestration:** How are these agents invoked? Sequentially? In parallel? Is there a meta-agent that decides which to use? The files define *what* they are, not *how* they are composed.
2.  **The `CLAUDE.md` Contract:** What happens when the agents disagree with the guidelines? Is there a feedback loop, or do they simply flag violations? The relationship feels one-way.
3.  **Tool Permissions:** Are `KillShell` and `BashOutput` sandboxed? The uniform toolset suggests a "run anywhere" model, which seems at odds with the careful, conservative philosophy of the `code-reviewer`.
4.  **Evolution:** The prior findings mention a denied claim about a `code-simplifier.md`. Does this triad represent a stable core, or is it one snapshot of a mutable set of agents?

### Closing
What I see is a minimalist, powerful, and somewhat dangerous framework. It builds specialized intelligence not by giving agents different tools, but by giving them different *procedures* and *values* (confidence thresholds, decisiveness). The system trusts the prompt to shape the tool, not the tool to shape the agent. The tension is between the uniformity of capability and the specialization of purpose. I would tell the next scout: look for the conductor. These are three instruments in an orchestra; find the score and the conductor. Also, find `CLAUDE.md`—it is the silent partner in every transaction.
