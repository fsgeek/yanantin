### **Strands**

#### **Strand 1: The Epistemic Graph and Tensors as Living Records**
- **What I saw:**
  - The `ots/` directory contains **1,500+ `.ots` files**, each presumably representing a "tensor" (a structured record of AI-generated knowledge). These files are named with **hexadecimal IDs**, suggesting a **unique, versioned, and possibly immutable** storage system.
  - The `scout_*/*.md` files (e.g., `scout_0001_20260212_gemma-2-9b-it.md`) are **commented with metadata** (cost, tokens, timestamps, verdicts) that **embeds the tensor’s provenance** directly into the text.
  - The `tensors.md` file in `src/yanantin/apacheta/` contains **Pydantic models** for `Tensor`, `Composition`, and `Provenance`, indicating that tensors are **structured, composable, and versioned** entities with **explicit lineage tracking**.

- **What it made me think:**
  - The project seems to treat **tensors as first-class citizens** in a **graph of knowledge**, where each tensor is a **node** with **edges representing composition, correction, or dissent**.
  - The **metadata-heavy approach** (costs, tokens, verdicts) suggests a **cost-conscious, auditable system**—perhaps an attempt to **quantify epistemic value** in terms of computational effort.
  - The **hexadecimal naming** of `.ots` files hints at a **distributed or sharded storage system**, possibly for scalability.

- **Confusions:**
  - Why are tensors stored as **both `.ots` and `.md`**? Are these **two representations of the same thing**, or is there a **transformation process** between them?
  - How are **duplicate or conflicting tensors** resolved? The `find_tensor_files()` function in `src/yanantin/apacheta/models.py` handles symlinks and deduplication, but what about **logical duplicates** (same content, different IDs)?
