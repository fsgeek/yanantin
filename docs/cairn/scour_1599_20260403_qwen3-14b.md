# Tensor Report: Examination of `src/yanantin/chasqui`

## Preamble
I was directed to examine the `chasqui` module, which forms the core of the Yanantin project's automated codebase analysis system. This module implements a distributed, cost-aware model dispatch system that scouts codebases, tracks coverage, and synthesizes insights through tensor-based reporting. My attention was first drawn to the interplay between `model_selector.py` and `coverage.py`, which together govern how models are chosen and how their exploration is prioritized.

---

## Strands

### 1. **Cost-Aware Model Dispatch (model_selector.py)**
The `ModelSelector` class implements a cost-weighted random selection algorithm, favoring cheaper models inversely proportional to their cost. This is critical for efficiency, as models like `gpt-3.5-turbo` (0.001/M) are selected 10x more often than `gpt-4` (0.03/M). However, the nominal 0.001/M cost for free models may create a bias toward models that are technically free but have hidden costs (e.g., rate limits, latency). The `exclude_patterns` mechanism allows filtering, but its effectiveness depends on the quality of pattern definitions.

**Connection to Project**: This aligns with Yanantin's dual focus on human-AI collaboration and cost efficiency. However, the system assumes that cost is the primary determinant of model utility, which may not hold in all contexts (e.g., when accuracy is more critical).

---

### 2. **Coverage Tracking and Stale Code Prioritization (coverage.py)**
The `coverage_weights` function assigns higher priority to files not reviewed recently, using epoch zero as a baseline. This addresses a critical flaw in the original scout dispatch system, where popular files were reviewed repeatedly while new code was ignored. However, the reliance on `basename` fallback matching (`_lookup_coverage`) could lead to false positives if multiple files share the same basename (e.g., `utils.py` in different directories).

**Assumption**: The system assumes that file names are unique enough to resolve ambiguities, which may not hold in larger projects. A potential improvement would be to use full paths with a fallback to hash-based identifiers.

---

### 3. **Claim Analysis and Quality Scoring (analyst.py)**
The `Analyst` class filters garbage claims, scores models, and clusters claims by semantic similarity. It uses word-overlap for clustering, which is deterministic but may miss nuanced semantic relationships. The `ModelProfile` quality score (40% ref ratio, 30% confidence, 30% garbage avoidance) is a balanced heuristic, but it doesn't account for contextual relevance of claims.

**What Could Break**: If models start generating claims with high ref ratios but low-quality content (e.g., trivial observations), the scoring system might incorrectly prioritize them. This highlights a gap between structural metrics and semantic value.

---

### 4. **Scout Prompt Engineering (scout.py)**
The `SCOUT_TEMPLATE` enforces a strict tensor format with sections like "Strands," "Declared Losses," and "Open Questions." This structure ensures consistency in output but may limit models' creativity. The inclusion of `gather_prior_findings` (which injects verified claims from the cairn) encourages scouts to build on prior work, but it also risks reinforcing biases in the cairn's existing claims.

**Missing Element**: There's no explicit mechanism to incentivize scouts to challenge prior findings or explore contradictions, which could lead to confirmation bias in the cairn.

---

### 5. **Verification and Epistemic Accounting (attestation.py)**
The `verdict_to_evaluation` function maps Chasqui verdicts (CONFIRMED, DENIED, etc.) to T/I/F epistemic scores. This bridges Chasqui's verification results with Willay's epistemic receipts. However, the declared losses (e.g., "Single-LLM verification") are hardcoded and don't adapt to the specific context of the claim being verified.

**Risk**: The system assumes that all verification is inherently limited by single-LLM checks, which may not be true for claims verified by multiple models or human reviewers.

---

## Declared Losses
- **Over-Reliance on Cost Metrics**: The model selection system may prioritize cheaper models over more accurate ones in scenarios where cost is not the primary concern (e.g., critical code reviews).
- **Ambiguity in Coverage Matching**: The use of `basename` fallback in coverage lookup may lead to incorrect prioritization of stale files.
- **Limited Semantic Clustering**: The Analyst's word-overlap approach may miss semantically similar claims that differ in phrasing.
- **No Mechanism for Challenging Prior Claims**: Scouts are encouraged to build on the cairn but not to question its assumptions, which could perpetuate errors.
- **Hardcoded Declared Losses**: The epistemic accounting in `attestation.py` does not allow for context-specific losses, reducing its adaptability.

---

## Open Questions
1. **How does the system handle models that produce high-quality claims but are expensive?** The current cost-weighting might undervalue such models unless explicitly overridden.
2. **Is the word-overlap clustering sufficient for capturing meaningful patterns in claims?** Could a hybrid approach (e.g., combining word-overlap with embeddings) improve accuracy?
3. **What safeguards exist to prevent the cairn from accumulating incorrect or biased claims?** The system relies on verification, but verification itself is limited to LLMs.
4. **How are file paths resolved across different projects or directories in external codebases?** The `coverage.py` fallback logic may fail in complex multi-repo scenarios.

---

## Closing
The `chasqui` module is a sophisticated, well-structured system for automated codebase analysis, with clear priorities around cost efficiency, coverage tracking, and structured reporting. It addresses critical challenges in ensuring that all parts of the codebase are reviewed, not just the popular ones. However, its reliance on deterministic metrics and hardcoded assumptions may limit its adaptability to edge cases or evolving project needs. For someone modifying this code, I would emphasize the importance of balancing structural heuristics with contextual flexibility, particularly in areas like model selection, claim analysis, and verification.
