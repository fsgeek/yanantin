**1. Immutability as Contract**  
The anchor service enforces immutability: every `update_cursor` and `flush` raises `ImmutabilityError` on duplicate UUIDs or handles. This aligns perfectly with the project’s emphasis on append-only, tamper-evident streams. The `MemoryAnchor` model is immutable post-creation; its `handle` never changes, only `cursors` and `timestamp` evolve. This guarantees temporal integrity — a fact record’s anchor remains consistent across views.

*Insight:* The pattern mirrors distributed ledger principles — anchors are state snapshots, not mutable entities. This ensures observers can trust the history without fear of alteration.

**2. Write Gate: The Two-Flag Paradox**  
The `flush()` method enforces a strict two-flag gate: both `updated` and `referenced` must be true to persist. This prevents premature writes (only `updated`) or orphaned anchors (`referenced` false). Yet this introduces a subtle dependency: anchors must be created *before* they’re flushed — a constraint not obvious from the interface. Missing documentation here risks misuse.

**3. Temporal Consistency & Provenance**  
Anchors track `provider_id` and `timestamp`, enabling precise temporal queries. The `materialize()` method reconstructs a view by fetching all latest facts per provider up to the anchor’s timestamp — a powerful but complex closure of time. This reflects the project’s focus on *epistemic observability*: every view carries metadata about its creation and scope.

**4. Anchor Lifecycle & Immutability Boundaries**  
`store_anchor` and `get_anchor` enforce strict lifecycle: once issued, anchors cannot be edited. `get_latest_anchor` returns the most recent unflushed anchor. `freeze()` authorizes a permanent tensor — a critical act, not just a query. This reveals a design where anchors are both ephemeral (created on demand) and permanent (frozen as tensor acts), embodying the project’s duality of flux and permanence.
