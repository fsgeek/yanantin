### Preamble
I was pointed at the `src/yanantin/chasqui` codebase — the heart of the messenger system in the Yanantin project. What drew my attention first was the deliberate duality: "scouts" wander freely while "scourers" have specific targets. The codebase embodies this duality in its structure and prompts. It's not merely a dispatcher; it's a sophisticated system for generating, tracking, and verifying knowledge about a codebase through AI-mediated observation.

### Strands

#### 1. The Messenger Metaphor is Deeply Embedded
**What I saw:** The entire system revolves around the "chasqui" (messenger) metaphor. Scouts "wander," scourers have "assignments," and reports are "tensors" — authored compressions of observation. This isn't just branding; it shapes the prompt design and output structure.

*   `scourer.py` defines different prompt templates based on scope (introspection, external, tensor analysis). Each template reinforces the messenger persona.
*   `coordinator.py` dispatches scouts and scourers, managing the "messengers" sent into the codebase.
*   `scout.py` includes prompt templates that explicitly tell the model to "wander" and "report what you find."

**What it made me think:** This metaphor is a powerful tool for guiding AI behavior. By framing the task as exploration and reporting rather than direct analysis or generation, it encourages the model to adopt a specific role. The quality of the metaphor's implementation across multiple files suggests a coherent design philosophy.

#### 2. A Multi-Stage Epistemic Pipeline
**What I saw:** The system implements a clear pipeline:
    1.  **Scout/Scour Dispatch:** Models are selected (cost-weighted) and given specific prompts (`coordinator.py`, `scout.py`, `scourer.py`).
    2.  **Report Generation:** Models produce "tensors" (Markdown reports) containing observations, declared losses, and open questions.
    3.  **Gleaning:** `gleaner.py` extracts structured claims from these reports.
    4.  **Analysis:** `analyst.py` processes these claims to find patterns, model quality, and topological insights.
    5.  **Verification:** `coordinator.py` can dispatch verifiers against claims, and `attestation.py` converts these into Willay epistemic receipts.

**What it made me think:** This shows a sophisticated understanding of how to generate and process knowledge. It's not just about asking an AI a question; it's about creating a system of record (the cairn) and then building tools to mine that record for verifiable insights. The explicit focus on "declared losses" at multiple stages (in scout reports, in analyst profiles, in attestations) is particularly noteworthy for maintaining epistemic honesty.

#### 3. Coverage-Driven Exploration
**What I saw:** `coverage.py` implements a coverage tracker that scans the cairn to see which files have been reviewed and when. This information is used by `scout.py` to weight file selection, prioritizing files that haven't been reviewed or have become stale. This is a feedback loop: past observations influence future exploration.

*   `coverage.scan_cairn_coverage` builds a map of `file_path -> last_reviewed_timestamp`.
*   `coverage.coverage_weights` uses this map to give higher weight to less recently reviewed files.
*   `scout.select_files_for_scout` uses these weights to pick files for the next scout.

**What it made me think:** This is a practical solution to a common problem in large codebases: ensuring coverage without manual effort. It makes the scouting process adaptive and ensures that new or changing parts of the codebase are more likely to be examined. The fallback to basename matching (`_lookup_coverage` in `coverage.py`) is a clever way to handle variations in path referencing within scout reports.

#### 4. Model Selection and Cost Awareness
**What I saw:** `model_selector.py` is dedicated to selecting models based on cost. Models are weighted inversely by their total cost per million tokens. Free or cheaper models are more likely to be selected, but the system still allows for more expensive models to be chosen sometimes.

*   `ModelSelector.load_from_openrouter_response` parses OpenRouter's model list.
*   `ModelSelector.select()` uses weighted random choice based on `1/cost`.
*   `ScoutProvenance` in `scorer.py` captures cost details for later analysis.

**What it made me think:** This reflects a pragmatic approach to operating at scale. By prioritizing cheaper models, the system can generate more data points (scout reports) for a given budget. The cost information is also captured, allowing for later analysis of cost-effectiveness (e.g., do more expensive models produce higher-quality reports or more verifiable claims?).

#### 5. Verifiability as a Core Principle
**What I saw:** The system is built around the idea of generating claims that can be verified.
*   `gleaner.py` extracts claims with file references.
*   `analyst.py` scores models partly based on the fraction of claims that have file references.
*   `coordinator.py` has `dispatch_verify_cairn` to send claims for verification.
*   `attestation.py` converts verification results into Willay epistemic receipts, which include truth/indeterminacy/falsity (T/I/F) scores and declared losses.

**What it made me think:** This is a significant step towards epistemic observability. Instead of just generating text, the system is designed to produce outputs that can be cross-checked and whose confidence can be assessed. The linkage with Willay (even if guarded by `try-except`) shows an intent to integrate with a broader epistemic accounting system. The `_VERDICT_EPISTEMICS` mapping in `attestation.py` is a key piece of this, translating discrete verdicts into continuous T/I/F values.

#### 6. Self-Awareness and Introspection
**What I saw:** The system can examine itself. The `--scour src/yanantin/chasqui` command in `__main__.py` allows a scourer to target the Chasqui codebase itself. The `introspection` scope in `scourer.py` is specifically designed for this. `analyst.py` also produces model profiles, enabling the system to "know" about the different AI models it dispatches.

**What it made me think:** This self-referential capability is powerful. It allows the system to monitor its own components, understand its biases (e.g., which models are good at what), and potentially adapt its own behavior over time. For instance, if the analyst finds that certain models consistently produce low-quality claims, the coordinator could be adjusted to de-prioritize them.
