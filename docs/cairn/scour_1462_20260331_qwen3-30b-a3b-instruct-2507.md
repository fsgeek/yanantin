### Closing  
The scouting system is **healthy in its core** — it reliably verifies file existence, function names, and basic imports. But it is **failing at deeper understanding**. The system is not just misreading code — it's **misreading meta-claims**.

**What works**:  
- The verification of simple, factual claims is robust and consistent.  
- Low-cost models often outperform high-cost ones on direct inspection tasks.  
- The network of claims creates a **self-correcting feedback loop** — false claims are eventually denied.

**What’s broken**:  
- The system is **over-indexing on meta-claims** — validating *that a claim was made* rather than *what the claim says*.  
- **High-cost models are creating noise**, not insight.  
- **Core architectural concepts** (provenance, composition, negation) are observed but not interrogated.

**Recommendation to maintainers**:  
1. **Audit the claims** — remove or flag claims that are self-referential or meta-recursive.  
2. **Prioritize deep dives** — assign scouts to analyze `ProvenanceEnvelope`, `negate`, and `correct` as *systems*, not just functions.  
3. **Enforce file completeness** — ensure `__main__.py`, `compose.py`, and `succession.py` are included in the cairn.  
4. **Reward precision over verbosity** — penalize reports that restate the claim in 10 paragraphs.  
5. **Create a "kernel" of verified facts** — use the 15 reports to build a **single, authoritative truth layer** that future scouts can reference, instead of re-reading 15 reports.

This is not a system of scouts — it’s a **distributed intelligence**. It can see the forest, but it hasn’t yet mapped the trees.
