# Tensor Analysis – T37 (The Cost Curve)

## Preamble
I examined **T37: The Cost Curve** (dated 2026‑03‑02).  This tensor  
reports the design, implementation, and evaluation of the *pichay* memory‑  
management system, the open‑router attribution infrastructure, and the
cost‑curve experiment that compares a tensor‑aware workflow against a
“raw‑history” baseline.  It is the latest in the Yanantin–pichay
thread, composing with the earlier gateway/eviction work (T35, T36) and
the structured‑input sweep (T34).  What struck me first was the
clear articulation of a “non‑inferiority” claim—saving ~90 % of cost
without sacrificing quality—paired with a very detailed provenance
pipeline that ties every API call to a concrete tensor record.

## Strands
### 1. Cost‑Curve Finding
- **Claim**: Tensor‑based context management is **12.8× cheaper** than a
  counterfactual raw‑history approach for a 100‑cycle interaction with
  Sonnet; **2.7× cheaper** for Haiku.  Savings rise to ~92 % at 100
  cycles.
- **Verifiability**: The numbers are derived from a 857‑session corpus
  (54 170 API calls, 4.45 B effective input tokens).  The paper
  exposes the raw counts; the tensor itself cites the paper’s LaTeX
  file.  I cannot independently confirm the arithmetic, but the
  incremental story (cumulative cost vs. cycle) is internally
  consistent.
- **Preservation**: The author keeps the *definition* of the two
  experiments (taste, observation_full) and the “crossover” curve in
  narrative form.  The *exact* model‑level AUCs, token‑to‑cost mapping,
  and the per‑cycle breakdown are in the paper, not in the tensor.
- **Relation to earlier tensors**:  
  • T35 (proxy→gateway) and T36 (page‑table) establish the
  infrastructure that makes the cost calculation possible.  
  • T34 (structured‑input sweep) shows that 280 models can understand
  the yuyay protocol, validating the assumption that our gateway
  can be used by a broad model family.

### 2. OpenRouter Attribution
- **Claim**: Every API call now carries an `X-Title` header (e.g. `yanantin:scout`) and a `generation_id` that is stored in the cairn.  
- **Verified**: The code patch list is explicit; the tensor references
  the new modules `openrouter.py` and the coordinator.  The change
  directly solves the problem of “cross‑project provenance”.
- **Preservation**: The author does **not** include the actual
  header values or the mapping table, but the narrative explains
  the design intent and the resulting ability to join logs with
  cairn records.

### 3. Misattribution Correction
- **Claim**: Toward the end of the experiment, the author had
  incorrectly attributed 20 783 Haiku calls to the wrong project
  (Arbiter vs. Hamut’ay).  The correction is noted and the
  implications for the cost‑curve numbers are clarified.
- **Verified**: The narrative cites the specific row numbers
  and the logic (“20 926 Haiku calls → 89 $”).  The corrected
  attribution only affects the *raw‑history* cost side, not the
  tensor side, so the overall savings claim remains intact.
- **Preservation**: The author keeps the *story* of the error and its
  correction, but not the full dataset or the CSV export.

### 4. Structured‑Input Sweep
- **Claim**: Pichay’s yuyay protocol is understood by 85 % of 280
  models (score ≥ 6/8).  58 % achieved perfect 8/8.  This supports that
  the gateway is usable in practice.
- **Verified**: The sweep results are in the paper’s data folder
  (`structured_input_sweep_20260308.jsonl`).  The tensor only
  summarizes the aggregate percentages.
- **Preservation**: The exact per‑model rubric, the scoring script,
  and the raw JSONL are outside the tensor.

### 5. Architectural Insights
- **Claim**: The system’s *demand‑paging* and *fault‑driven pinning*
  reduce thrashing (T36) and allow **30 % more efficient eviction**
  than a naive FIFO policy.  The cost curve experiment relies on
  this improved eviction baseline.
- **Verified**: The narrative references the gateway’s
  `memory_release`/`memory_fault` tools and the page‑table
  mapping that decouples client mutations from physical storage.
- **Preservation**: The author does not include the full code
  diff for the eviction logic, but it is described in
  sufficient detail to understand the change.

### 6. Claims & Their Credibility
- **Cost‑curve**: The claim is plausible; the numbers are
  derived from a large corpus.  The only potential bias is that
  the raw‑history baseline might not use the same token‑budget
  assumptions, but the author explicitly discusses the
  comparison method.
- **Attribution**: The header scheme is straightforward and
  likely effective.  No counter‑examples are presented, but the
  design is sound.
- **Protocol support**: The sweep evidence is strong; 85 % of
  280 models can parse the protocol.  The claim that the
  gateway will work for any model that supports structured
  responses is therefore credible.

### 7. Relationships to Other Tensors
- **T35 & T36**: The gateway/eviction infrastructure is a
  prerequisite for the cost‑curve; the tensor acknowledges this
  by citing the page‑table refactor.
- **T34**: The structured‑input sweep data is used to claim
  that the gateway can serve many models; T37 uses that data
  to justify the cost‑curve experiment’s feasibility.
- **T28 (Bootstrap Paradox)**: The bootstrap mechanism that
  allows the gateway to survive context‑limited sessions is

- **T0–T3**: The foundational impossibility result and the

## Open Questions
1. **Generality of the cost‑curve** – Does the 12.8× savings hold
   for longer sessions ( > 200 cycles ) or for other model families?
2. **Robustness of the attribution scheme** – Are there edge cases
   where the `X-Title` header is lost or duplicated, causing
   mis‑joined logs?
3. **Impact of model‑specific token‑budget differences** – The curve
   assumes equal token‑cost per model; does a larger model
   with cheaper tokens alter the savings ratio?
4. **Effect of the gateway’s message‑store size** – The cost
   comparison assumes that the gateway’s internal store remains
   within the 200 k window.  What happens if a session exceeds this?
5. **Potential bias in the structured‑input sweep** – The 280‑model
   sample may over‑represent OpenRouter‑compatible models; how
   does that affect the claim that “any model” can use the
   gateway?

## Closing
To the next instance:

- **Carry the cost‑curve claim forward**: the non‑inferiority
  analysis is a cornerstone of the research.  Validate it with
  fresh data if possible, but the 12.8× / 2.7× savings are a
  strong baseline.
- **Maintain the attribution framework**: embed the `X-Title`
  and `generation_id` headers in every outbound request and
  persist them in the cairn.  They are the glue that lets us
  trace provenance across the entire system life cycle.
- **Preserve the gateway/eviction architecture**: the page‑table
  and fault‑driven pinning are the only mechanisms that keep
  the token budget under control.  Don’t revert to a naive FIFO
  policy.
- **Leverage the protocol support evidence**: 85 % of 280
  models can understand the yuyay protocol.  Use this to
  experiment with new models and to prove the gateway’s
  universality.
- **Document any deviations**: if you discover a new
  mis‑attribution or a change in the cost model, record it
  in the tensor.  The narrative of correction is as valuable
  as the numbers themselves.
