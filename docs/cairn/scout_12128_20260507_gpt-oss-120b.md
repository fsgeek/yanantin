### Closing  

The unit‑test forest paints a picture of a system that **formalises tensors as immutable, versioned knowledge packets** while **allowing the packet payload to evolve arbitrarily**. The developers have built a *safety net* of exhaustive contract tests: schema isomorphism, naming normalization, provenance parsing, and CLI output validation. The underlying tension—**rigid verification vs. open extensibility**—is managed by leaning on Pydantic’s flexible config and by embracing neutrosophic uncertainty.

For the next scout venturing deeper (e.g., into `src/yanantin/`), I would advise:

- **Inspect the Pydantic model configs** to see exactly how immutability and extra‑field allowance coexist.  
- **Run a schema dump** (`Model.model_json_schema()`) for each collector to understand the baseline the isomorphism test protects.  
- **Read the actual normalisation and extraction functions** to confirm they handle edge‑cases not covered by the tests.  
- **Check how coverage weights are computed**, as this influences the scout’s “vantage” selection and could be a hidden bias.  
- **Verify the provenance parser’s resilience** by feeding malformed comment blocks.

Overall, the test suite is a strong guardrail that reveals the project’s philosophical commitments: *truth is optional, provenance is mandatory, and tensors must be both immutable and extensible.* The next scout should keep an eye on any drift between those ideals and the concrete implementations.
