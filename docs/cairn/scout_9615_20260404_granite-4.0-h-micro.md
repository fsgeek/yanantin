### Strands
#### 1. **Temporal Abstraction & Persistence**
The `FactRecord` and `AnchorCursor` models define the fundamental units of the activity stream. `FactRecord` captures raw observations with a UUID identifier, provider reference, timestamp (normalized to UTC), and arbitrary data payload. The `AnchorCursor` model represents a provider's position in the stream, storing a cursor handle, a timestamp, and a tuple of `AnchorCursor` objects representing the provider's history. This abstraction implies that the system treats time as a Lamport clock, where each anchor advances only when the provider reports new data, ensuring a total order of events without relying on wall‑clock time.

#### 2. **Immutability & Persistence Guarantees**
Both models are defined with `frozen=True`, indicating that once instantiated, their state cannot be altered. This aligns with the project's emphasis on epistemic observability and the need for a reliable, audit‑ready record of activity. The immutability of `AnchorCursor` instances suggests a design choice to prevent accidental or malicious tampering with the stream's history, which is crucial for trust and reproducibility in epistemic systems.

#### 3. **Backend Agnostic Interface**
The `ActivityStreamStore` abstract base class outlines a contract for storing and retrieving facts and anchors. The three concrete backends (`InMemoryActivityStreamStore`, `DuckDBActivityStreamStore`, and `ArangoDBActivityStreamStore`) implement this contract, demonstrating a strategy pattern for persistence. The decision to keep the backends separate from the core models (imported directly rather than imported at package level) suggests a desire for flexibility: developers can choose the most appropriate storage solution without modifying the core activity logic. However, this also introduces a dependency inversion layer that could increase the cognitive load for new contributors unfamiliar with the abstraction.
