### Preamble
I am a scout dropped into the Dropbox nexus of the Yanantin project. While previous scouts focused on the *mechanics* of decomposition (the "what"), I am drawn to the *dualism* of the recording strategy (the "how"). The system isn't just collecting data; it is preparing a fight between snapshots and streams.

### Strands

**Strand 1: The Granularity Schism (Tensor vs. Fact)**
There is a profound tension between `recorder.py` and `fact_recorder.py`. 
- `DropboxRecorder` (lines 37-84) treats the entire Dropbox listing as a single, heavy, two-strand `TensorRecord`. It preserves the "state of the world" at a specific moment.
- `DropboxFactRecorder` (lines 43-68) shatters that world into individual `FactRecord` atoms.
This isn't just a difference in scale; it's a difference in philosophy. One records a *landscape* (the listing); the other records *events* (the entries). The system assumes you will need both the holistic context and the granular heartbeat, but it doesn't yet show how these two different temporal resolutions reconcile when they meet in the `ApachetaInterface`.

**Strand 2: The Invariant Guardrails**
The `models.py` file is surprisingly aggressive in its enforcement of reality. The `_check_invariants` method in `DropboxEntryData` (lines 46-62) and `DropboxListing` (lines 87-104) suggests a deep distrust of the upstream API. 
The system doesn't just accept "data"; it validates that folders have zero size and that path casing is consistent. This implies that Yanantin views "epistemic observability" as something that must be protected from the "noise" of malformed or inconsistent external truths. It is building a "clean room" for data.

**Strand 3: Deterministic Hallucination**
`synthetic.py` is more than a testing utility; it is a mirror. By using `_fake_content_hash` (lines 38-44) and `_fake_rev` (lines 47-51), the system creates a "shadow Dropbox" that is mathematically predictable. 
The use of `paretovariate(1.5)` (line 84) to generate file sizes is a sophisticated touch—it mimics the "heavy-tail" distribution of real-world file systems (many small files, few massive ones). This suggests the developers aren't just testing for "does it work," but "does it behave like a real, messy universe."

**Strand 4: The OAuth2 Friction Point**
`collector.py` reveals a pragmatic, almost "human-in-the-loop" vulnerability. The `_interactive_auth` flow (lines 114-132) requires a human to copy-paste a code from a browser. This creates a hard break in what otherwise looks like a highly automated, composable pipeline. The system is "composable" until it needs to prove its identity, at which point it becomes a manual, social process.

### Open Questions
- **The Reconciliation Problem:** If `DropboxRecorder` stores a snapshot and `DropboxFactRecorder` stores a stream, how does the system detect if a "fact" is a duplicate of something already contained within a "tensor"?
- **The Cursor Paradox:** `DropboxListing` carries a `cursor` for incremental sync (line 77), but the `DropboxRecorder` treats the whole listing as a single tensor. Does the system intend to store a new, massive tensor for every incremental update, or is the cursor meant to feed the `FactRecorder` exclusively?
- **Identity Collision:** `DropboxEntryData` uses `content_hash` from Dropbox (line 28), but `DropboxFactRecorder` generates its own SHA-256 hash (line 68). Which one is the "true" identifier for a fact?

### Closing
The Yanantin Dropbox collector is not a simple scraper; it is a dual-mode observer. It is designed to capture both the "Big Picture" (Tensors) and the "Small Truths" (Facts). It is highly defensive about data integrity, which tells me the system's ultimate goal is high-fidelity reasoning.
