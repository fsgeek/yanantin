### Preamble

As `google/gemini-2.0-flash-001`, the sheer *volume* of `docs/cairn` files
caught my eye first. It's a chaotic garden of scout reports, evals,
and model outputs. I chose to focus on patterns in the `scout_` and
`scour_` reports, especially concerning model attributions and dates, as
well as their relationship to the `pipeline_health.json` file. It felt
like a good entry point to understanding the "epistemic observability"
aspect of the project.

### Strands

#### 1. Llama's Lingering Shadow: Recurring Model Mentions

I noticed a striking recurrence of `llama-3` variants across a large
swath of the `docs/cairn` scout reports. Skimming through filenames,
I see models like:

*   `scout_0002_20260210_llama-3-8b-instruct.md`
*   `scour_0014_20260213_llama-3-8b-instruct.md`
*   `scour_0023_20260214_llama-3-8b-instruct.md`
*   `scout_0040_20260212_llama-3-8b-instruct.md`
*   ...and many more.

This suggests a heavy reliance on Llama models, particularly around
February 2026, at least for the scouting process. This might be a
reflection of the model's cost-effectiveness, performance, or licensing
at that time. It makes me wonder about the project's sensitivity to
model availability and how the model selection process works in practice.

#### 2. The 2026-02-12 Anomaly: A Burst of Scout Activity

Looking at the dates in the `docs/cairn` filenames, I noticed a
significant cluster of activity around `2026-02-12`. There are hundreds
of `scout_` and `scour_` reports bearing that date. This raises a few
questions:

*   Was there a specific event or experiment that triggered this surge?
*   Does this represent automated, systematic testing, or a more ad-hoc
    exploration?
*   How does this peak correlate with the data in `pipeline_health.json`?

I'm curious to see if this date aligns with any significant events or
milestones documented elsewhere in the project, perhaps in the
`docs/cairn` directory outside of the scout reports themselves.

#### 3. A "Liquid" Theme: Recurring Model References

I'm seeing "liquid" appear in the model names, specifically
`liquid/lfm2-8b-a1b`. This makes me suspect there's a partnership or
specific focus on models from LiquidAI. This would be a good thing to
corroborate by looking at the source code, maybe specifically in the
`src/yanantin/chasqui/model_selector.py` file (though I didn't actually
look in the file).

#### 4. The Tale of Two Scout Reports: `scout_` vs. `scour_`

The `docs/cairn` directory contains both `scout_` and `scour_` reports.
My understanding (without deep diving) is that `scout_` reports are
likely generated by the "chasqui scout" agents, while `scour_` reports
are related to the `scourer.py` module. This naming convention suggests
different processes or levels of analysis within the Yanantin system.

I'd expect the `scour_` reports to be more detailed, perhaps containing
analysis or interpretations based on the raw data collected by the
`scout_` agents. However, without inspecting the code, this is just a
guess.

#### 5. OTS Everywhere: A Stamp of Trust?

The sheer number of `.ots` files in the `.claude/ots` directory is
staggering. "ots" probably stands for OpenTimestamps, and from
`claude/hooks/ots_stamp.py`, I gather this is some kind of notarization
or timestamping system. The presence of so many OTS files suggests a
strong emphasis on verifying the integrity and provenance of data within
the Yanantin system. It would be interesting to understand how these
timestamps are used in practice and whether they are exposed through the
project's API.

### Closing

Overall, my impression is that the Yanantin project is a complex system
with a strong emphasis on data integrity and provenance. The sheer volume
of data in the `docs/cairn` and `.claude/ots` directories is both
impressive and somewhat overwhelming.

If I were to brief the next scout, I would suggest they focus on:

1.  **Connecting the data**: Correlating the `docs/cairn` scout activity
    with the data in `pipeline_health.json` to understand the system's
    overall health and performance.
2.  **Tracing the data flow**: Following the data from collection to
    analysis to understand how the scout and scourer agents interact and
    how the OTS timestamps are used to verify data integrity.
3.  **Understanding model selection**: Investigating the
    `src/yanantin/chasqui/model_selector.py` module to understand how
    the models are chosen for scouting and scouring.

I'd also emphasize the importance of understanding the project's
terminology and design principles, as this seems crucial for interpreting
the data and drawing meaningful conclusions.
