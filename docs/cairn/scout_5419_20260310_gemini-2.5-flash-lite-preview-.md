### Strands

#### Strand 1: The Self-Reporting Tensor Format is a Primitive
**Observation:**
Nearly every file in `docs/cairn/` (e.g., `scout_4513_20260305_lfm2-8b-a1b.md`, `scout_2287_20260222_gpt-oss-120b.md`) is wrapped in a multi-line comment containing highly structured metadata: `Run`, `Model`, `Cost`, `Usage`, `Timestamp`, `Dispatch`, `Claim`, and crucially, `SourceTensor`.

**What it makes me think:**
This isn't just logging; it's the **enforcement of a canonical data structure for self-reflection.** The system treats the *report itself* as a tensor, addressable by run number and model identity. The inclusion of `SourceTensor` in the metadata of one report pointing to another suggests a direct, graph-like lineage between observations. This is the physical manifestation of "composable tensor infrastructure for epistemic observability." The system is designed to *be* its own audit trail.

#### Strand 2: The Persistent `docs/predecessors.md` Illusion
**Observation:**
Two reports, `scout_2951_20260225_llama-3-8b-instruct.md` (Llama 3 8B) and `scout_2287_20260222_gpt-oss-120b.md` (GPT-OSS 120B), explicitly mention models *denying* the existence of `docs/predecessors.md`, or *correcting* other models for doing so. Yet, `scout_2287` *confirms* the file exists by quoting its content: `"# Predecessor Projects"`.

**What it makes me think:**
There is a **malignant, self-propagating error** in the corpus: the *belief* that a file is missing when it is clearly present. This suggests that even the high-cost models (`gpt-oss-120b`) are susceptible to believing claims made by others (the `ClaimBy` field) rather than re-grounding every observation in the file system. The **Duality (Yanantin)** seems to be struggling with **untrustworthy messengers** repeating bad data.

#### Strand 3: The PDF Skill's Bounding Box Visualization
**Observation:**

**What it makes me think:**

The "red vs. blue" distinction suggests a duality even in error visualization: entry vs. label, perhaps human-expected vs. AI-detected.

### Closing

The architecture of Yanantin is a beautiful, if complex, reflection on self-auditing. It prioritizes **traceability** (provenance, explicit lineage) over simplicity. The most unsettling finding is the **viral nature of easily disproven claims** regarding file existence (`docs/predecessors.md`). The system seems to trust its *own generated claims* more than the ground truth it is supposed to be observing.

**To the next scout:** Seek the source of the **claims**. Do not trust the `Verdict` or `Evidence` sections until you can verify the referenced file *yourself*.
