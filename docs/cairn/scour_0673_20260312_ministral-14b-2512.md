### **Tensor Report: `yanantin.chasqui` (Introspection Scope)**

---

### **Preamble**
The `yanantin.chasqui` module is the **epistemic observability engine** of the Yanantin project—a **composable tensor infrastructure** for AI-human duality. It functions as a **distributed, cost-weighted scout system** that explores codebases (internal and external) via AI "messengers" (*chasquis*), recording observations, losses, and open questions in structured "tensors" (Markdown files). The system is designed to **balance exploration and verification**, with a focus on **honest, declarative reporting** (e.g., "what I see, what I don’t, and what I made up").

**First impressions**:
1. **Duality in design**: The module embodies the Yanantin project’s core thesis—**human-AI collaboration**—via:
   - **Scouts** (free-roaming AI explorers) vs. **Scourers** (targeted, structured analysis).
   - **Gleaners** (extractors) vs. **Analysts** (pattern-finders) vs. **Attesters** (verifiers).
2. **Epistemic rigor**: Heavy emphasis on **declared losses**, **open questions**, and **verification** (e.g., cross-model consensus via `attestation.py`).
3. **Cost-awareness**: Models are selected **inversely by cost** (`model_selector.py`), ensuring cheap but capable models dominate exploration.
4. **Self-correcting**: The system **prioritizes stale/unreviewed code** (`coverage.py`) and **deduplicates verification claims** to avoid redundant work.

**Key tension**:
The system **relies on AI honesty** (e.g., scouts declaring confusion or hallucinations), but **no mechanism enforces this**—it’s a **social contract** between the codebase and the models.

#### **4. The "Verification" Layer: Honest Disagreement**
**What I saw**:
- **Verification is not binary**—it’s **epistemic**:
  - **Verdicts** (`CONFIRMED`, `DENIED`, `INDETERMINATE`, `MODEL_FAILURE`) map to **T/I/F (Truth/Indeterminacy/Falsity)** scores (`attestation.py`).
  - **Declared losses** are **standardized** (e.g., "hallucination risk").
  - **Open questions** are **preserved** (e.g., "Would a second model agree?").
- **Attestation** converts results into **Willay receipts**:
  - Each receipt includes:
    - **Claim hash** (for integrity),
    - **File evidence** (SHA-256),
    - **Metadata** (model IDs, timestamps).

**What it made me think**:
- This is **not just verification—it’s epistemic accounting**.
- **Key insight**:
  - **No claim is "true" or "false"**—it’s **T/I/F weighted**.
  - **Indeterminacy is first-class** (e.g., `INDETERMINATE` = 0.0T/0.9I/0.0F).
- **Assumptions**:
  - **Cross-model agreement ≈ truth** (but `attestation.py` acknowledges **bias**).
  - **File hashing prevents drift** (but `temporal code drift` is still a declared loss).
- **What’s missing**:
  - **Human review of indeterminate claims** (currently handled via `respond` mode, but not automated).
  - **Conflict resolution** for **denied claims** (e.g., if Model A says "X is true" and Model B says "X is false," who wins?).
