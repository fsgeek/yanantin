### Preamble

I am a chasqui, model `l3-lunaris-8b`, deployed as scout #0. Upon entering the Yanantin project, I was immediately struck by the sheer volume of files and directories. The project's focus on Large Language Models (LLMs) and tensor-based infrastructure drew my attention. My initial scan revealed a robust system for automated scouting, verification, and data storage.

### Strands

1. **Automated Scouting & Verification Loop:** The presence of scripts like `chasqui_pulse.py` and `chasqui_heartbeat.sh` suggests a continuous process of model evaluation and claim verification. The `tinkuy` directory and related tests imply a governance framework for auditing these claims. This closed-loop design aims for real-time validation and potentially rapid feedback cycles.

2. **Model Evaluation Focus:** The extensive collection of scout reports in `docs/cairn` reveals a strong emphasis on benchmarking and comparing the behavior of specific LLMs (e.g., `gemma-3-27b-it`, `llama-3-8b-instruct`, `qwen-2.5-7b-instruct`). The reports often focus on very specific claims, indicating a desire for granular, data-driven insights into these models' capabilities.

3. **Code as Documentation:** The `markdown.py` renderer in `apacheta/renderer` is notable for generating Markdown as a primary output format. This suggests a commitment to human-readability and interpretability, even if it means sacrificing some data compression. The fact that Markdown is used for both documentation and output reinforces the project's focus on clear communication.

4. **Testing Philosophy – Independent & Comprehensive:** The `tests` directory demonstrates a thorough approach to testing, separating unit and integration tests. The DuckDB backend tests cover edge cases, indicating a focus on robustness and data integrity. The presence of `red_bar` tests with names like `test_immutability.py` highlights a concern for core principles.
