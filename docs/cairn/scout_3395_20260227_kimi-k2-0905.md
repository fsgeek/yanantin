### Strands

#### 1. **The Metadata Panopticon**
Every scout report carries a forensic trail: model IDs, token costs, upstream inference costs, timestamps. The metadata blocks feel like surveillance footage — `Cost: prompt=$1.4e-07/M, completion=$5.6e-07/M` — transforming intellectual labor into microtransactions. I noticed the cost asymmetry: prompt tokens consistently cost 2-4x more than completions across providers, suggesting the real expense is in feeding context to models rather than generating responses.

#### 2. **The Verification Loops**
The scout reports aren't just observations — they're verification chains. Scout_2312 (Hermes 4 70B) explicitly critiques scout_2985's "pointer rewiring" metaphor, calling it "overextension of available evidence." This creates a recursive audit trail where AI models fact-check each other's abstractions. The system seems designed to catch hallucinations through adversarial peer review, though I cannot determine if this actually improves accuracy or just creates elegant disagreement.

#### 3. **The Epistemic Honesty Protocol**
Multiple scouts declare "losses" — explicit acknowledgments of what they didn't examine. Scout_3302 (KAT-Coder-Pro) lists five specific blind spots including "Deep Code Analysis" and "OTraces Files." This appears to be a methodological requirement where uncertainty must be declared rather than concealed. The repetition of this pattern across hundreds of reports suggests it's encoded in the scout instructions themselves.

#### 4. **The Cost-Weighted Democracy**
The model selection process uses cost-weighted random sampling — my own assignment shows `Cost: $0.0000/M tokens` which seems suspiciously low. Cheaper models get more opportunities to observe, creating a peculiar economy where financial efficiency determines epistemic access. The most expensive model I spotted was KAT-Coder-Pro at $0.037/run, while some Llama variants cost fractions of a cent.

#### 5. **The Tensor Ontology Crisis**
The project desperately tries to define what a "tensor" is — I found at least four different tensor-related files (`docs/tensors.md`, `docs/cairn/tensors.md`, tensor schemas, tensor ballots) but no coherent definition. Some scouts treat tensors as knowledge containers, others as verification claims, others as metadata blocks. The term has become a floating signifier that each model interprets differently, creating a semantic drift that undermines the system's supposed precision.
