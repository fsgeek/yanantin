### Strands  

#### 1. **Constrained vs. Open Prompts**  
The `find_by_author.yaml` and `find_by_lineage.yaml` are *constrained* prompts, explicitly directing the model to use specific filters (e.g., "scout-7" or "iteration_v1"). In contrast, `find_a_record.yaml` is *open*, requiring the model to choose a filter autonomously. This tension suggests the system is testing **flexibility vs. precision**: does the model need guidance to act, or can it infer the right approach?  

The open prompt’s ambiguity is intriguing. Why is the author_instance_id hardcoded as "scout-7"? Is this a placeholder for a real identifier, or a test case? The answer might lie in the tools’ implementation, but the YAML files don’t reveal that.  

#### 2. **Lineage Tags as Metadata**  
All three prompts reference **lineage_tags**, which are described as properties of records. This implies the system treats metadata as first-class, possibly for tracking provenance or versioning. The `find_by_author.yaml` asks for the number and content of lineage_tags, suggesting they are both *data* and *signals* for further filtering.  

But why are lineage_tags plural? Are they hierarchical, or do they represent distinct categories? The lack of examples in the YAML files leaves this unclear. This could be a design choice to keep the system general, but it also introduces ambiguity.  

#### 3. **Tool Call Chaining**  
The `find_by_author.yaml` explicitly requires the model to **chain a tool call** (find a record) and then **reason over its result** (count lineage_tags). This hints at a system where tools are modular, and the model must act as a coordinator. However, the YAML files only describe prompts, not the actual tools. Are the tools implemented elsewhere, or are these prompts meant to be parsed by a separate system?  

The absence of tool definitions in the files raises questions about the system’s architecture. Are these prompts meant to be executed by a runtime, or are they just test cases?
