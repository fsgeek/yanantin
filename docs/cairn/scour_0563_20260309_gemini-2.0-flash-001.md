I was directed to examine the `src/yanantin/chasqui` directory, which contains the modules responsible for dispatching scouts and scourers, analyzing their reports, and managing the overall workflow of the Yanantin project's exploration of its own codebase. The consistent use of "chasqui" (messengers) as a metaphor is immediately striking, as is the emphasis on structured outputs ("tensors") and epistemic observability.

### Strands

*   **Modularity and Responsibility:** The directory is well-organized, with each file representing a distinct component of the scouting and analysis pipeline. `coordinator.py` acts as the central dispatcher, `scout.py` and `scourer.py` define the behavior of the AI agents, `gleaner.py` extracts claims, `analyst.py` distills insights, `scorer.py` evaluates the quality of scout reports, and `attestation.py` converts verification results into epistemic receipts for Willay. This separation of concerns suggests a deliberate effort to create a maintainable and extensible system.
*   **Epistemic Awareness:** The project is deeply concerned with the limitations and biases of AI models. `analyst.py` includes functions to detect "garbage" output and score model quality, while `attestation.py` defines "declared losses" to account for the inherent uncertainties in single-LLM verification. The emphasis on "honest T/I/F values" (Truth, Indeterminacy, Falsity) in `attestation.py` shows a commitment to transparency and acknowledging the limits of AI-generated knowledge.
*   **Coverage-Driven Exploration:** `coverage.py` introduces a mechanism for tracking which files have been reviewed by scouts and prioritizing those that haven't. This coverage-driven approach is crucial for ensuring that the entire codebase is explored and that new code doesn't get overlooked. The use of `activity_map` alongside `coverage_map` in `scout.py` to boost recently-changed files highlights a sophisticated approach to balancing exploration and adaptation to change.
*   **Model Selection and Cost Optimization:** `model_selector.py` focuses on selecting models based on cost, with cheaper models being dispatched more frequently. This indicates a practical concern for resource management and a willingness to trade off model quality for cost-effectiveness. The `seed` function in `ModelSelector` suggests an awareness of the need for reproducibility in experiments.
*   **Willay Integration (Guarded):** The `attestation.py` module interacts with Willay to record epistemic receipts, but all Willay imports are guarded by `try...except` blocks. This allows the rest of the `chasqui` package to be used even if Willay is not installed. This design decision demonstrates a commitment to modularity and avoiding unnecessary dependencies.
*   **Tensor Structure and Provenance:** The use of "tensors" as the primary output format for scouts and scourers is a key design choice. The prompt templates in `scout.py` and `scourer.py` define a consistent structure for these tensors, including sections for "Preamble," "Strands," "Declared Losses," "Open Questions," and "Closing." The provenance header (parsed by `scorer.py`) provides detailed metadata about the scout's configuration and resource usage.
*   **Verification and Consensus:** The `coordinator.py` module includes logic for deduplicating verification reports and limiting the number of verifications for the same claim. This suggests an awareness of the potential for verification cascades and a desire to establish consensus among different models. The `analyst.py` module identifies "topological" claims (those supported by multiple models) as indicators of structural truth.
