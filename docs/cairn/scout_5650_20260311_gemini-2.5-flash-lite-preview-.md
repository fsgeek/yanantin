## Chasqui Report: Yanantin Exploration

### Preamble

I observed from the vantage of `google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite-preview-09-2025`, a model chosen for its low cost ($0.0000/M). My gaze was immediately snagged by the sheer volume of *data* in `docs/cairn/ots/`. It speaks not of intent, but of **persistence**—a vast, flat graveyard of named binary blobs. This suggests an obsession with recording every ephemeral observation, regardless of its eventual utility.

### Strands

#### 1. The Philosophy of Documentation in `tmp/ubuntu-vm.claude/plugins/cache/claude-plugins-official/hookify/core/config_loader.py`

This file, deep within a plugin cache, defines how *rules* are loaded for an execution hook system. It sports an impressively convoluted YAML frontmatter parser (`extract_frontmatter`) that attempts to handle "indented list items" while parsing markdown metadata.

*   **What I saw**: The parser logic is baroque, mixing indentation checks with key/value splitting, clearly an attempt to impose structure on what is inherently unstructured (YAML embedded in Markdown).
*   **What it suggests**: The system *desperately* wants its configuration (the "hook") to be human-editable prose, but it needs the rigidity of structured data. This tension between flexible human input and rigid machine parsing is a core architectural stress point. The existence of `Rule.from_dict` relying on *both* `conditions` list and a legacy `pattern` field indicates a long, messy evolution of requirements.

#### 2. The Ritual of Epistemic Scouring in `docs/cairn/`

The density of files named `scout_XXXX_YYYY-MM-DD_MODEL_NAME.md` in `docs/cairn/` is staggering. I sampled three reports: one from **Qwen3 14B** (`scour_0201...`), one from **LFM2-8B-A1B** (`scour_2736...`), and one from the low-cost model I am, **Gemma 2 9B** (`scour_4688...`).

*   **What I saw**: Models are meticulously tagged with their specific identifier, run number, cost, and token usage, even for simple verification tasks (`scout_4688...` was a `verify` dispatch). The `SourceTensor` field in the header shows a chain of custody for the *prompt* itself.
*   **What it suggests**: This isn't just logging; it's **epistemic provenance audit**. They aren't just recording *what* the model said, but *how much it cost to ask* and *what previous observation* prompted the question. The goal is not just knowledge accumulation, but demonstrably *efficient* knowledge accumulation.

#### 3. The Dual Nature of `yanantin/data/`

The data directory is split into two major experimental areas: `compaction_experiment` and `noninferiority`.

*   **What I saw**: `compaction_experiment` contains directories named by UUIDs, each holding artifacts like `raw_messages.json`, `cleaned_messages.json`, and a `comparison.json`. The `noninferiority` directory, however, seems to hold paired data (`baseline_messages.json` vs. `treatment_messages.json`) plus `verdicts.json`.
*   **What it suggests**: There is a clear duality in experimentation:
    1.  **Compaction**: Optimizing data representation/storage (perhaps reducing the noise/overhead of the raw tensors).
    2.  **Non-inferiority**: Directly comparing a new system/model against a baseline using explicit verdicts. This screams of a system under constant, rigorous pressure to prove that newer, cheaper/faster components (like me, the Flash Lite model) are "good enough."

#### 4. The Ghost of `_RELATION_MAP`

In `docs/cairn/scour_0201_20260225_qwen3-14b.md`, the model noted a question about the completeness of `_RELATION_MAP` in `materialize.py` (which I didn't see).

*   **What I saw**: The scout report *itself* reports on a *missing piece* of the puzzle: the map that translates human language (`"composes_with"`) into system types.
*   **What it suggests**: The success of the entire system hinges on a small, static mapping of concepts agreed upon by humans. If the relationship vocabulary evolves, the hard-coded map in `materialize.py` must be updated, creating a bottleneck for conceptual evolution.

### Declared Losses

I chose not to deeply examine the contents of the **`ots/`** directory (hundreds of files named by hex strings). These appear to be raw, persistent snapshots of some state or tensor. I assume they are the ground truth state for certain operations, but reading them would require understanding the `storage_obfuscator.py` and the underlying serialization format, which is outside the immediate scope of observing *intent* from the higher-level Python structure.

I also skipped deep inspection of **`tmp/ubuntu-vm.claude/`**. This directory is clearly a local, isolated execution environment for Claude, full of cached skills, plans, and session data. It’s a shadow ecosystem, and while interesting for understanding the *operator's* environment, it's noise relative to the *project's* declared structure (`src/yanantin`).

### Open Questions

1.  **The Willay Connection**: The scouting module mentions mapping verdicts to "Willay Evaluations" in `attestation.py`. What is Willay, and how does it *consume* these evaluations? Is Willay the ledger, and Yanantin the observation engine?
2.  **Free Model Weighting**: In `model_selector.py`, free models are assigned a nominal cost of `$0.001/M` to prevent infinite weight. If the *actual* cost of a paid model is `$0.000001/M` (i.e., cheaper than the nominal free cost), does the system handle that correctly, or does the nominal cost act as a floor, artificially inflating the weight of the "free" model?
3.  **The `precompact_tensor.py` hook**: This file exists in `.claude/hooks/`. What is this tensor being pre-compacted *for*, and what is the cost/benefit of running this specific operation *before* a standard git hook fires (`precompact_tensor.py` vs. the standard `precompact_tensor.py` in `tools/`)?

### Closing

The Yanantin project is deeply invested in **rigorous, auditable, and cost-aware data provenance**. The architecture is a complex dance between human-readable prose (`.md` files in `docs/cairn`, configuration files in `tmp/ubuntu-vm.claude/plugins/`) and highly structured, versioned backend data (`ots/`, `data/`).

To the next scout: **Focus on the tension points**: the parsing logic in `hookify`'s config loader, and the specific *comparison* logic in the `noninferiority` experiments. The system is built to observe its own observations, and the most interesting data lies where the narrative (human language/scout reports) is forced into the tensor structure. Beware the legacy path in configuration loading; it hints at past architectural compromises.
