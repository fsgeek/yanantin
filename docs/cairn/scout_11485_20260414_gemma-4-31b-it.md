### Preamble
I observed from the periphery of a plugin directory (`55b58ec6e564`), a cached instance of a `code-review` tool. My attention was immediately seized not by the files, but by the *orchestration*. This is not a script; it is a recipe for a synthetic bureaucracy.

### Strands

**The Synthetic Hierarchy**
In `commands/code-review.md`, I see a rigid caste system of LLMs. "Haiku" is the clerk—handling eligibility, gathering files, and scoring confidence. "Sonnet" is the specialist—the one tasked with the actual intellectual labor of the review. This reveals an assumption that intelligence is a tiered resource to be spent precisely: use the cheap model to filter the noise, and the expensive model to find the signal.

**The Confidence Filter (The 80% Threshold)**
The most surprising tension is the "Confidence Scoring" (Steps 5 & 6 of `code-review.md`). The system acknowledges that AI is prone to hallucination or "nitpicking" (as seen in the "Examples of false positives"). To solve this, it creates a recursive loop: an agent finds a bug, and then a *different* agent (Haiku) must judge the confidence of that finding. The hard cutoff at 80 is a fascinating admission of fragility. It suggests that the system would rather miss a real bug than annoy a human with a false positive.

**The Documentation as Law**
The obsession with `CLAUDE.md` is striking. It is treated as the "Source of Truth" for style and intent. The agents are not just reviewing code; they are auditing the code's adherence to a specific text file. This transforms the `CLAUDE.md` from a helpful guide into a legal contract that the AI enforces.

**The Divergence of Truth**
I noticed a discrepancy between the `README.md` and the `code-review.md` command. The `README.md` claims the tool launches **4** parallel agents (Agents #1 & #2 grouped for CLAUDE.md), but the actual command logic in `code-review.md` (Step 4) explicitly launches **5** parallel Sonnet agents, each with a distinct specialized role (Audit, Bug Scan, History, PR Context, and Comments). The README is a simplified map; the command is the actual terrain.

### Declared Losses
I ignored `plugin.json` and the `LICENSE`. They are administrative boilerplate. I did not attempt to trace where these "Haiku" and "Sonnet" agents are actually defined or invoked, as I am confined to the plugin's definition files and not the execution engine that parses them.

### Open Questions
*   **The Ghost in the Machine:** Who writes the `CLAUDE.md` files? If the AI is auditing based on `CLAUDE.md`, and a human (or another AI) writes the `CLAUDE.md`, the system is a closed loop of stylistic reinforcement.
*   **The Scoring Logic:** How does a Haiku agent "verify" a bug found by a Sonnet agent? If Sonnet is the more capable model, why is the "judge" a less capable one? This seems like a potential bottleneck for accuracy.
