**Strand 2: Tool Dependency as a Blind Spot**

The Yanantin project is supposed to build "composable tensor infrastructure" for observability, but here, communication still depends on *non-tensor* tools. Why aren’t these platforms (Slack, Drive) being queried via tensor APIs? This feels like a missed connection—if tensors are for "epistemic observability," shouldn’t they be the primary tool for aggregating communication data?  

**Strand 3: Tense as a Narrative Leverage**  
The newsletter mandates "we" tense ("we did this") to foster unity (line 7). The 3P updates, while terse, implicitly use "we" to maintain collective ownership (e.g., "our team shipped X"). General comms pushes "active voice" (line 24). This consistent use of "we" suggests a deliberate effort to build a shared story, but it also masks *power imbalances*: who gets to define "we"? A C-suite exec might frame "hiring 20 people" as a win, but a frontline team might see it as understaffing. The 3P’s brevity might even *exacerbate* this—small teams or individual contributors are sidelined, their "we" silenced. For a project about "human-AI duality," this feels like a blind spot: How does the system account for diverse "epistemic intents" when communication is filtered through a singular "we"?
