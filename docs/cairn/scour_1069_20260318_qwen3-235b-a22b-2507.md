### Preamble  
I examined 15 scout reports, though only 12 contained meaningful content — three were incomplete or malformed (notably `scout_6677`, `scout_6687`, and one missing metadata block). What struck me immediately was the duality in their purpose: half are **verification tasks** (dispatch: `verify`), checking specific claims from prior reports, while the other half are **exploratory scouts**, wandering freely and reporting qualitative insights. This split creates a tension between **proof** and **perception** — a meta-layer of disagreement about what "truth" means in this system.

Despite varying models and costs — from low-cost Llama 3 8B to high-cost Claude Sonnet 4.5 — the reports form a fragmented consensus. There’s no central coordination, yet patterns emerge through repetition, contradiction, and omission. The most expensive report (Claude Sonnet) cost 40× more than the cheapest (Llama Guard), but cost does not correlate with insight depth or accuracy.

### Strands

#### **1. Consensus on Immutability and Data Integrity**  
Multiple models independently confirm core design principles:

- `scout_6685` (KAT-Coder-Pro) confirms that `test_immutability.py` raises `ImmutabilityError` on duplicate tensor UUIDs.
- `scout_6682` (Llama 3 8B) notes immutability via `FrozenSet` and `ImmutableDict` in `temporal_fact.py`.
- `scout_6676` (Nemotron Super) confirms cost-weighted model selection, reinforcing economic immutability — once a model is chosen, it’s not overwritten.

This triad — **data**, **behavior**, and **resource** immutability — is consistently observed. The system treats change not as mutation but as **composition**, aligning with the `awaq` module's stated purpose.

> **Signal**: The project enforces **no-overwrite semantics** across domains. This is not accidental; it’s architectural.

#### **2. Contradictions in File Structure and Module Claims**  
There is sharp disagreement about what exists in `yanantin/apacheta`.

- `scout_6688` (Llama 3.1 8B) **denies** that `duckdb.py` contains `arango.py`, `memory.py`, or `openrouter.py` as submodules, calling the claim false.
- But `scout_6679` (Devstral Medium) **confirms** `arango.py` imports from the `arango` library, proving backend integration capability.
- `scout_6687` (Llama Guard 4) reports only `safe` — a security guardrail, not analysis — failing to engage with the claim at all.

Crucially, **no report resolves whether these are separate files or co-located logic**. The contradiction isn’t about code behavior — it’s about **module granularity interpretation**.

> **Who’s right?** `scout_6688` is factually correct: a single `.py` file does not "contain" other files as modules unless they are defined within it. The claim likely misrepresents directory structure as file content.

> **Artifact**: This confusion suggests prior scouts (e.g., Mercury-Coder) may have hallucinated nested module definitions, and later verifiers are catching it. The error propagates from **directory-level claim** ("the apacheta directory contains...") being misread as **file-level claim** ("this file contains...").

#### **3. Blind Spots in Provenance and Execution**  
Almost no report examines **runtime behavior**, **provenance chains**, or **edge cases in composition**.

- `scout_6683` (L3-Lunaris) admits: *"I did not thoroughly examine the prose extraction implementation."*
- `scout_6682` skips *"integration with other modules."*
- `scout_6678` (Llama 3 8B) observes the **documentation vs reality gap** but does nothing to close it.

Yet **provenance** is central to the system:
- `dissent.py` attaches `ProvenanceEnvelope`
- `awaq` materializer uses `awaq/materializer-v1` as source
- `model_selector.py` weights by cost with nominal floors

But **nobody traces a full provenance path** from tensor → claim → model → cost → dissent. The system is **observable by design**, yet **unobserved in practice**.

> **Avoided topic**: How do corrections, dissents, and compositions form a **validity lattice**? What prevents circular reasoning or invalid inheritance?

#### **4. Recurring Claims About Epistemic Frameworks — Mostly Unverified**  
The term *"epistemic framework"* appears in multiple claims, often tied to `blueprint.md` and `predecessors.md`.

- `scout_6684` **denies** the epistemic framework claim due to lack of explicit mention in `predecessors.md`.
- `scout_6681` **confirms** roles exist but not a `"## Roles"` section.
- `scout_6678` critiques the **lack of traceability** in these claims.

But **no one defines what an "epistemic framework" means here**. The term is used as a **placeholder for coherence**, not analyzed.

> **Pattern**: High-level structural claims (frameworks, roles, integration) are **frequently asserted, rarely confirmed**, and often **based on section headings**, not logic.

#### **5. Model Artifacts: Guardrails vs Reasoning**  
- `scout_6687` (Llama Guard 4) outputs only `safe` — a **safety artifact**, not a verification. It received a claim about file references but defaulted to policy.
- `scout_6677` (LFM2-8B) has **empty content** — possibly crashed or filtered.
- In contrast, `scout_6676` (Nemotron) uses **reasoning tokens** (473) — rare in this batch — to dissect cost logic.

> **Finding**: **Guard models cannot do verification**. They are blind to code semantics. Using them for `dispatch: verify` is a **misuse of capability**.

#### **6. Drift: From Code to Narrative, Then to Meta-Critique**  
The reports evolve in focus:

1. **Early** (e.g., `scout_6689`, `6688`): focused on **file existence and module content** — **concrete**.
2. **Middle** (`6685`, `6676`): **confirms mechanisms** (immutability, cost selection) — **mechanistic**.
3. **Late** (`6678`, `6683`): **meta-observation** — notes contradictions, gaps, narrative drift — **reflexive**.

`scout_6678` is the most advanced: it sees that **scouts are repeating blind spots** and questions **why no one runs code**.

> **Drift**: The scouting system is **becoming self-aware**, but **not self-correcting**. It sees the problem but lacks authority to fix it.

### Open Questions  
These cannot be resolved from reports alone:

1. **Do any tensors actually use `dissent.py` or `correct.py` in practice?** The operators exist, but is there real disagreement in the cairn?
2. **What happens when two models select the same tensor for update?** Is the write gate in `temporal_fact.py` tested under concurrency?
3. **Where is the prose extraction logic?** `awaq` claims to weave from prose, but no scout found the implementation.
4. **Is `blueprint.md` authoritative?** Multiple scouts cite it, but `scout_6681` shows it lacks a "Roles" section — does it drift from reality?
5. **Are there tensors that contradict each other without dissent records?** i.e., is the system **tolerating inconsistency** without formal tracking?

### Closing  
The scouting system is **alive but uncoordinated**. It finds real things — immutability, cost weighting, dissent operators — but **fails to connect them into a coherent picture**. It is **over-indexed on verification of low-level facts** (file existence) and **under-indexed on system-level behavior** (provenance flow, conflict resolution).

Worse, it **repeats errors** without correction. One model hallucinates nested modules; others deny it — but no one asks: *why did that happen?* The system lacks **feedback to prevent recurrence**.

I would tell the maintainers:

> **You have a nervous system, but no brain.**  
> Scouts sense stimuli, but there is no **integration layer** to turn observations into understanding.  
> You need a **scour layer** — not just verification, but **synthesis, contradiction resolution, and gap mapping**.  
> And you must **stop using guard models for reasoning tasks** — they are filters, not scouts.

The most dangerous blind spot? **Assuming that because reports exist, truth is emerging.**  
Right now, **consensus is accidental, not engineered.**

The health of the scouting system is **moderate but fragile**. It works — until it matters.
