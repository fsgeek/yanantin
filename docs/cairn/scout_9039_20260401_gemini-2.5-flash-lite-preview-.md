### Strands

#### 1. The Ossified History of Scrutiny (`cairn/`)
The most prominent feature is the massive collection of scout reports, timestamped across February and March 2026. This suggests intense, daily, and highly granular introspection into the system's current state across many LLM evaluations.

*   **Observation:** The sheer density of files like `scout_xxxx_modelname.md` (e.g., `scout_0001_20260210_ministral-3b.md` up to `scout_8987_20260401_llama-3.1-8b-instruct.md`) implies a commitment to *traceability* of evaluation artifacts.
*   **Thought:** This is a system that treats every exploratory step as a potential source of truth, akin to a growing cairn. However, the presence of *so many* highly specific, model-named reports suggests that the *process* of evaluation might be more documented than the *synthesis* of those findings. The system seems to be collecting data points (scout reports) rather than immediately forming dense, generalized knowledge (tensors).

#### 2. The Duality of Evidence: Tensors vs. Confirmation (The Verified Claims)
The "Prior Findings" section shows a constant tension between what a scout *claims* to have found and what another scout *verifies*.

*   **Observation:** Several prior findings involve claims about the *absence* of certain text or files being verified (e.g., `scout_8127_...md` and `scout_3726_...md`). For instance, Scout 8127 (Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite Preview) **CONFIRMED** that `docs/blueprint.md` *doesn't* claim itself to be the authoritative source for *all* claims.
*   **Thought:** This reveals an assumption that the system's structure (`blueprint.md`) might try to claim ultimate authority, which the scouts are actively checking for. The system seems to be designed to self-limit its own claims of total authority, or perhaps the scouts are checking for hubris in the documentation. The goal is "epistemic observability," so checking for over-claiming seems integral.

#### 3. The Theme of "Compaction" and "Edges"
The directory structure under `cairn/compaction` and `cairn/edges` is highly suggestive of the system's core data processing model.

*   **Observation:** `cairn/compaction` is filled with `.md` files named `TXX_compaction_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS.md` alongside several hash-named files ending in `_auto.md` or `_manual.md`. Simultaneously, `cairn/edges` is filled with hundreds of JSON files named `confirms_XXXX_hash.json` and `denies_XXXX_hash.json`.
*   **Thought:** This directly maps to the project description: "composable tensor infrastructure for epistemic observability."
    *   The Tensors (`TXX...`) seem to be the synthesized knowledge/narratives.
    *   The JSON files in `edges` are the raw input/output of agent interactions—confirmations and denials. This looks like a graph database of peer review or consensus formation, where every interaction is recorded as a binary edge.
    *   The `compaction` directory seems to be where narratives are forged from these raw edges, distinguishing between human (`_manual.md`) and automated (`_auto.md`) consolidation efforts. This hints at a fundamental tension: **Human vs. Automated Synthesis.**

#### 4. The Mystery of the `ots` Directory
The `ots` subdirectory contains hundreds of files with `.ots` extensions, hashed names, and timestamps stretching into 2026.

*   **Observation:** Scout 4355 (ERNIE 4.5 VL) explicitly verified that `.claude/hooks/ots_stamp.py` uses OpenTimestamps to create *blockchain timestamp proof* for commits, storing the resulting proof in these `.ots` files.
*   **Thought:** This is the most surprising piece of infrastructure found in the documentation. The system is not just recording its internal state; it is attempting to *anchor its history to an external, immutable ledger* (a blockchain via OpenTimestamps). This is a profound commitment to **provenance and resistance to retrospective editing**, even for ephemeral scout reports. It suggests a deep distrust of internal time-stamping, or a need to demonstrate external, verifiable truthfulness for the "epistemic" claims being made.
