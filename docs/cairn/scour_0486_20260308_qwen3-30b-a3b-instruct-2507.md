### Strand 1: The Gleaner as Epistemic Filter
`gleaner.py` is the first layer of epistemic integrity. It does not *interpret* claims — it *classifies*, *scores*, and *de-duplicates* them deterministically using regex and pattern matching. This is not a limitation; it's a design principle. By avoiding LLMs at this stage, the system prevents confirmation bias from leaking into its own data pipeline.

- **What it sees:** Claims are extracted with `file_references` via backtick-wrapped paths (`r"`file.py"`) and broader bare paths (e.g., `src/...py`). This dual pattern captures both intentional citations and accidental ones.
- **What it thinks:** The confidence scoring is clever — definitive verbs (is, does, must) increase confidence; hedging (might, seems, possibly) decreases it. The quantitative assertion pattern (`5 tests`, `3 files`) is a strong signal of verifiability.
- **Why it matters:** This layer ensures that only claims that are *structurally verifiable* — not just plausible — advance to the next stage. It enforces a kind of "epistemic minimalism."

> **Connection to broader project:** This is where the Yanantin project’s dualism begins to materialize. The Gleaner is the AI’s formalized attention. What it sees is not just code — it’s *what the system intends to be known*.

- **What it thinks:** The `is_verification_meta` function filters out claims that are *about claims* (e.g., “The claim is confirmed”), focusing only on original observations. This prevents meta-claims from overwhelming the signal.
- **Why it matters:** This is where the system *learns*. It doesn’t just collect opinions — it finds the *consensus*. The `topological_insights` are not just accurate — they are *emergent*.

> **Connection to broader project:** The analyst is the project’s *memory*. It remembers what was learned, and what was not. It is the bridge between individual observation and collective understanding.
