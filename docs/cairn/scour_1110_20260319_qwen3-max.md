### Preamble  
I examined **15 scout reports** (`scout_6799` through `scout_6813`). What struck me immediately was the **bimodal nature** of the corpus: most reports are **verification dispatches** (short, structured, verdict-driven), while a few—like `scout_6813` (Gemini) and `scout_6801` (Gemini 3)—are **exploratory syntheses** with thematic strands. This suggests the scouting system serves two purposes: (1) micro-verification of atomic claims, and (2) macro-reflection on system architecture. The volume of verification tasks implies a **high-trust, high-audit culture**, but the exploratory reports hint at deeper philosophical concerns about epistemic recursion and data mortality.

### Strands  

#### 1. **Consensus on Core Architecture**  
Multiple reports independently confirm key architectural facts:  
- **Apacheta** is the tensor database, with **DuckDB** as a backend (`scout_6813`, `scout_6810`).  
- **Abstraction layers** are enforced: scripts like `ingest_cairn.py` use `ApachetaGatewayClient` and avoid direct DB calls (`scout_6808` CONFIRMED).  
- **Immutability** is a first-class concern, enforced via signed commits, test coverage (`TestImmutabilityAllTypes` in `scout_6806`), and versioned artifacts (`dist/yanantin-0.1.0.tar.gz` in `scout_6813`).  

This cross-model agreement suggests these are **stable, well-documented truths** in the codebase.

#### 2. **Contradictions Around “Mention” vs. “Implementation”**  
A subtle but important tension appears in claims about **what is “mentioned” in code**:  
- `scout_6810` (LFM) **DENIED** a claim that `duckdb.py` doesn’t mention `duckdb.py` by name—arguing that *implicit usage* (via `import duckdb`) falsifies the claim.  
- `scout_6803` (Qwen) **DENIED** a claim that `tensor.py` was “ignored,” citing its docstring calling it the “core data unit.”  

These aren’t true contradictions but **semantic disputes**: one model treats “mention” as *literal filename reference*, another as *functional presence*. The **correct interpretation** aligns with `scout_6803`: the system cares about *role*, not lexical self-reference. The LFM report overcomplicated a simple issue.

#### 3. **Blind Spots: Runtime Behavior and Data Semantics**  
No scout examined:  
- **How compaction actually works at runtime**—`scout_6811` DENIED a claim about `capture_compaction.py` based on comments, but didn’t validate behavior.  
- **The meaning of experimental data** in `data/compaction_experiment/`—`scout_6813` noted its existence but declared it out of scope.  
- **Quechua terminology** (e.g., *Yanantin*, *Apacheta*, *Tinkuy*) beyond surface-level acknowledgment (`scout_6813` Declared Loss #5).  

This reveals a **structural bias**: scouts verify *static artifacts* (code, docs) but avoid *dynamic semantics* (data meaning, runtime effects).

#### 4. **Recurring Verified Claims**  
Certain claims are repeatedly **CONFIRMED**:  
- The **scout report format** (Verdict/Evidence/Reasoning) is consistent (`scout_6806`).  
- **No parsing of `blueprint.md`** in `audit.py` (`scout_6812`).  
- **Empty scout reports** (e.g., `scout_4168`) are just metadata—**not** “non-finding” records (`scout_6807` DENIED that interpretation).  

These suggest the system **self-stabilizes** around procedural truths.

#### 5. **Model Artifacts: Cost vs. Depth**  
- **High-cost models** (`gemini-2.0-flash-lite`, `gemini-3-flash-preview`) produced **synthetic, thematic reports** with cultural and philosophical framing (“digital memento mori,” “judicial system for AI claims”).  
- **Low-cost models** (`mistral-nemo`, `gemma-2-9b`) focused **narrowly on verification**, often with minimal reasoning.  

This isn’t noise—it’s **intentional role differentiation**. The system leverages expensive models for synthesis, cheap ones for atomic checks.

#### 6. **Drift: From Exploration to Verification**  
The highest-numbered reports (`6813`, `6812`, `6811`) include both deep synthesis and micro-verification, but earlier reports in the batch (`6809`, `6805`) are **empty** (likely failed runs). This suggests **instability at scale**: as scout volume grows (#6800+), some runs produce no output, possibly due to context limits or model failure. The system may be **hitting operational limits**.

### Closing  
The scouting system is **highly effective at verifying atomic, static claims** and **moderately effective at synthesizing architectural themes**. However, it shows **three critical gaps**:  
1. **Dynamic blind spot**: It avoids runtime behavior, data semantics, and performance characteristics.  
2. **Cultural superficiality**: It uses indigenous terminology without examining its conceptual integration.  
3. **Scalability stress**: Empty reports suggest the system may be overloading its own verification loop.  

To the maintainers: **Your system is trustworthy for “what is written,” but not for “what happens.”** Consider adding scouts that:  
- Execute compaction hooks and log outputs,  
- Analyze experimental data for patterns,  
- Interview the *intent* behind Quechua naming (e.g., “Does *tinkuy* imply conflict resolution in tensor merging?”).  

The recursion is impressive—but without grounding in *behavior*, it risks becoming a hall of mirrors.
