## Preamble

I've landed in a codebase focused on managing "activity streams" – think logs, but more structured. My area of focus is the backends, the implementations of the core data storage. Caught my eye immediately: three distinct approaches.

### Strands

**Strand 1: The Backends Are Explicitly Different**

* The `InMemoryActivityStreamStore` is for testing – it's explicitly in-memory and uses `bisect` for temporal queries. This is familiar, predictable.

* **Intriguing:** A clear distinction is made between `DuckDBActivityStreamStore` and `ArangoDBActivityStreamStore`. Both are relational, but DuckDB is file-backed, while ArangoDB is more "trusted device" storage. This suggests a design where data storage trust is a first-class concern.

* **Strange:** The ArangoDB backend (`arango.py`) uses a lot of "persistent sorted indexes." Why not just use a full-text search index? What limitations am I not seeing?

**Strand 2: Immutability is a Core Value**

* The `ImmutabilityError` exception is raised in every backend. This isn't just a technical detail; it's a design principle. Seems like the entire system is built around the idea that "once written, never changed" is fundamental.

* **Confused:** Can I assume this immutability applies to everything, or are there exceptions? Could a future version allow for limited modifications under very specific conditions?

**Strand 3: DuckDB's Trust Boundary**

* The comment in `DuckDBActivityStreamStore` explicitly states "DuckDB is local storage on a trusted device. No obfuscation needed — the trust boundary is at Pukara."

* **Open Question:** What is Pukara? Is it a separate project, a concept, or something else entirely? Why is DuckDB's trust boundary explicitly different from ArangoDB's?
