### Preamble

As Gemini 2.0 Flash, I dove into the `docs/` directory, expecting a
relatively static collection of documentation. What surprised me was the
extent to which these documents seem to be *actively* used and even
*generated* by other parts of the system. The sheer volume of "scout"
reports, for example, suggests a system that's constantly interrogating
itself.

### Strands

1.  **The Cairn is a Living Archive:** The `docs/cairn` subdirectory is
    more than just a record; it appears to be a dynamically updated
    repository of system introspection. The `compaction` subdirectory,
    with its mix of "auto" and "manual" Markdown files dated in the
    future (2026!), implies an ongoing process of summarizing and curating
    AI-generated observations. This raises the question: *Who* or *what*
    is doing the "manual" compaction, and what criteria are they using?

2.  **Scout Reports as Epistemic Currency:** The `scour_NNNN_...md` files,
    with their consistent naming convention and model attributions, suggest
    a standardized reporting format. What's intriguing is the sheer *number*
    of these files, covering a diverse range of models (llama, gemma, qwen,
    etc.) and runs. This indicates a systematic effort to compare and
    contrast different models' views of the codebase, treating their
    outputs as a kind of epistemic currency. *What mechanisms are in place
    to resolve conflicting claims from different models?*

3.  **Edges of Confirmation and Denial:** The `docs/cairn/edges` directory
    contains JSON files named `confirms_*` and `denies_*`, pointing to a
    structured system for tracking relationships between claims. It's
    interesting that there are FAR more `confirms` than `denies` files.
    Is this because the system is generally accurate, or because the
    "denies" edges are handled differently (e.g., rolled up into a
    compaction report)? The asymmetry is a data point. *What is the
    process for handling conflicting or contradictory claims?*

4.  **The Recurring Date of 2026:** The dates in many filenames (e.g.,
    `T37_20260328_the_cost_curve.md`, in the `docs/cairn` directory and
    within the scout reports) are consistently in the future. This could
    be a stylistic choice, a placeholder, or a subtle indication of the
    project's intended lifespan or target deployment date. It also raises
    a practical question: *How will the system handle the transition when
    2026 actually arrives?*
