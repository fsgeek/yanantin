Strands
- Strands of immutability and schema-agnostic storage
  - What I see: ActivityStreamStore defines a uniform contract for two kinds of records (FactRecord and MemoryAnchor) that are append-only and immutable. Facts carry a generic data dict and a content_hash; anchors carry a handle, a timestamp, and a collection of Cursor data. This is codified in store.py and echoed in __init__.py per the module docs.
  - Why it matters: The system is built to trust the provenance of raw observations (metrics/data) while keeping anchors as lightweight, versioned cursors. The contract enforces a fixed write pattern (no updates, no deletes) across backends.
  - Tension/curiosity: The exact meaning of content_hash and how it’s produced isn’t visible here, but it’s wired into the store implementations (DuckDB shows it, Arango maps it). I wonder how content_hash integrity is ensured across backends and what mounts the hash to actual data provenance.

- The two-flag write gate in MemoryAnchorService
  - What I see: In anchor.py, MemoryAnchorService uses an updated flag (providers reported new data) and a referenced flag (someone asked for the current position). flush persists only if both are true; materialize resolves an anchor against current streams; freeze pins a temporal view into a tensor via an ApachetaInterface.
  - Why it matters: This design avoids thrashing writes for anchors and ensures a consumer explicitly requests progress before persisting. It’s a thoughtful synchronization primitive across the fact stream and the tensor store.
  - Tension/curiosity: It’s very process-local: the flags live in memory. I’m curious how this behaves under multi-worker or multi-process deployments. If a process dies after updated but before referenced, anchors might never persist. The code hints at a single-writer, single-consumer mental model, or at least an expectation that the caller coordinates these actions carefully.

- Late-binding resolution and ephemeral views
  - What I see: The materialize() method fetches the current anchor, then queries all providers (list_providers()) to assemble facts up to the anchor timestamp. It then builds an AnchorView with a late-bound set of providers.
  - Why it matters: This allows the view to reflect providers added after the anchor’s creation, ensuring the view isn’t “frozen” to a fixed provider set. It supports a dynamic epistemic view where newer providers can participate in a given anchor’s resolution.
  - Tension/curiosity: The approach requires iterating all providers to resolve facts at resolution time. Depending on scale, this could be expensive. It raises questions about incremental/materialized views or caching, which aren’t evident here.

- Data models and timestamp discipline
  - What I see: FactRecord timestamps are normalized to UTC via _ensure_utc; Anchor timestamps also normalize to UTC. Anchors are identified by a UUID handle and carry a timestamp; FactRecord carries a content_hash and arbitrary data payload.
