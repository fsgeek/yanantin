**Strand 1: Model Immutability & Data Integrity**

*   `models/base.py` defines `ApachetaBaseModel` with `frozen=True` in its `ConfigDict`. This means that once a model instance is created, its attributes cannot be changed.
*   The `ser_json_bytes="base64"` configuration in `ApachetaBaseModel` suggests that binary data can be serialized and deserialized using base64 encoding.
*   The combination of immutability and explicit serialization handling points towards a system designed for data integrity and potentially for secure storage or transmission of tensor data. This also indicates a bias against in-place modification, favoring the creation of new records to represent changes.

**Strand 2: Explicit Provenance Tracking**

*   `models/provenance.py` introduces `ProvenanceEnvelope` which includes `SourceIdentifier`, `timestamp`, `author_model_family`, `author_instance_id`, etc. This envelope is intended to wrap every record, answering "who made this, when, from what context."
*   The `predecessors_in_scope` field in `ProvenanceEnvelope` (a tuple of UUIDs) suggests the system tracks dependencies between records. This allows tracing the lineage of a tensor and understanding its derivation.
*   `EntityResolution` in `entities.py` also includes a `ProvenanceEnvelope`, indicating that even entity mappings are versioned and auditable.

**Strand 3: Epistemic State Representation**

*   `models/epistemics.py` defines models for representing the epistemic state of claims, strands, and tensors. The use of `truth`, `indeterminacy`, and `falsity` as independent floats (not constrained to sum to 1) is interesting. As the docstring notes, this is neutrosophic logic.
*   The `RepresentationType` enum (SCALAR, FUNCTIONAL) suggests that epistemic values can be represented in different ways. A "functional" representation likely involves a function or model that computes the epistemic value, rather than a single scalar. But, the meaning of `functional_spec` is opaque.
*   The `LossCategory` enum details why information might be lost (CONTEXT\_PRESSURE, TRAVERSAL\_BIAS, AUTHORIAL\_CHOICE, PRACTICAL\_CONSTRAINT). This suggests a system aware of its own limitations and biases. The inclusion of `TRAVERSAL_BIAS` hints at graph traversal algorithms used to construct tensors.

**Strand 4: Composition & Dissent**

*   `models/composition.py` contains records for different types of compositions: `BootstrapRecord`, `CorrectionRecord`, `DissentRecord`, `NegationRecord`, and `SchemaEvolutionRecord`. This indicates that tensors can be built through various operations, including correcting errors, registering dissent, and handling schema changes.
*   The presence of `DissentRecord` and `NegationRecord` explicitly acknowledges the possibility of disagreement and contradiction within the tensor data. This is crucial for epistemic observability, as it allows the system to represent and reason about conflicting information.
*   `RelationType` in `models/composition.py` suggests that the edges in the composition graph are typed.

**Strand 5: Entity Redaction**

*   `EntityResolution` in `entities.py` has a `redacted` field. Setting this to `True` effectively removes the ability to resolve a UUID to an identity, but *without* touching the tensor records themselves.
*   The docstring emphasizes "privacy-as-architecture." This suggests that the system is designed to be privacy-preserving by default, allowing for the selective removal of personally identifiable information (PII) without affecting the underlying data.
